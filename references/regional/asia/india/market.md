# Market: India

Loaded on demand when the target market is India only (private sector, public sector/PSU, campus and lateral hiring).

## At a glance
- Paper: A4.
- Photo: not required by any source found; the government NCS CV builder invites one (« A clear, professional photo increases your chances of getting noticed »; a form hint, not a rule) and has no marital status or date of birth field (National Career Service, « CV builder », consulted 2026-11-07, S548); Naukri's own resume template still carries a passport-size photograph placeholder (Naukri (Info Edge), « Resume template », undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S99). Tell the user the custom; the candidate decides. Default omit for private-sector/MNC/ATS targets (skill default, LOW, unsourced).
- CV language: English for private sector and MNCs. Government/PSU postings may require an application form in the posting's language, not a free-form CV.
- Personal data: private sector, omit all but contact. Government/PSU and some campus formats use fuller personal details (father's name, marital status, gender) (LOW, unsourced). Never a national ID (Aadhaar, PAN) or passport number.
- Length: 1 page fresher/campus, 2 pages experienced (LOW, unsourced; no authoritative source).
- Formality / verb style: English; no India-specific verb convention found.

## Scope
Thinly sourced: official bodies publish little CV guidance, so most custom rules below are labelled LOW, unsourced; ask the user which segment they target.

Candidate with Indian credentials targeting another market: also read [`india/outbound/anglosphere.md`](outbound/anglosphere.md) (North America, UK, Australia, NZ) or [`india/outbound/europe-common.md`](outbound/europe-common.md) (Germany, Ireland, Netherlands, France).

## Resume vs biodata
- « Resume »/« CV » and « biodata » are used loosely. A biodata is the older, personal-details-first format (birth date, parents, marital status, religion, height). Build a resume unless the posting or an application form explicitly asks for a biodata or a declaration (LOW, unsourced on the boundary).
- Naukri's template ends with a « Declaration » (details correct, employer may verify; date, place, signature) and a Personal Details block (date of birth, nationality, addresses) (Naukri (Info Edge), « Resume template », undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S99). Treat both as optional legacy blocks: include only when the target expects them and the candidate agrees; a declaration adds nothing for ATS.

## Degree names and grading
- Naukri's template lists education from the highest qualification down to Class XII and Class X, each with board/university and percentage or CGPA (Naukri (Info Edge), « Resume template », undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S99). Campus and fresher CVs routinely keep Class X and XII results; experienced CVs drop them.
- B.E. vs B.Tech.: both are four-year engineering bachelor's degrees; the name is the awarding university's choice, and, for faculty recruitment in technical institutions, AICTE leaves the appropriateness of qualifications to the recruiting agency (AICTE, circular of 23.10.2020, « Clarifications on equivalence/relevance or appropriateness of nomenclatures of degrees for recruitment/promotion of teachers in technical institutions », snapshot 2020-11-02, S586: « The appropriateness of the qualifications must be naturally and relevantly decided by the recruitment agency keeping in view the nature of the program for which the recruitment is being carried out »; read from the scanned page, PDF p. 9, quote checked on a transcription of that page; the circular does not say B.E. = B.Tech). The four-year and university-choice claims rest on a secondary source (Careers360, « AICTE clarifies stand on degree equivalence in appointments », 2020-11-02, consulted 2026-11-07, S103). Write the exact name on the diploma and never normalise one into the other.
- Grades: write the figure as awarded with its scale (« CGPA 8.4/10 » or « 78% »). Do not convert CGPA to percentage yourself: universities use different formulas (UGC's CBCS guidelines say « The conversion from marks to letter grades and the letter grades used vary widely across the HEIs in the country » and give no CGPA-to-percentage formula; UGC, « UGC Guidelines on Adoption of Choice Based Credit System », undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S520). Use the formula printed by the candidate's university, or leave the original figure.

## Protected titles
- Chartered Accountant / « CA »: only a member of ICAI may use the designation or the « CA » prefix; passing the Final examination alone does not qualify (ICAI, « Pre-conditions for using the designation Chartered Accountant », 2009-08-07, consulted 2026-11-07, S100; ICAI, « Use of designation(s) other than the designation of Chartered Accountant », 2015-02-18, consulted 2026-11-07, S101; both citing Chartered Accountants Act 1949, s. 7). A CA-Final-passed non-member or a student writes « CA Intermediate » / « CA Final (cleared) » only if true, never « CA ».
- Architect: only a registered architect or firm may use the title (landscape and naval architects excepted); contravention is an offence (Architects Act 1972, s. 37, as reproduced by Advocatekhoj, « Architects Act, 1972, s. 37 Prohibition against use of title », consulted 2026-11-07, S102). Software, solutions and enterprise architect titles: no source settles these; flag, do not rule.
- « Chartered Engineer (India) » is a title the Institution of Engineers (India) (IEI) confers on its Corporate Members: « Every person being at any time a Corporate Member of the Institution may so long as he shall be a Corporate Member take or use the name or title of “Chartered Engineer (India)” » (Institution of Engineers (India), Royal Charter of 13 August 1935, Declaration 16, archived PDF, snapshot 2015-04-03, S587). IEI's current page says the charter is « protected under Article 372 of the Constitution of India » (Institution of Engineers (India), « Chartered Engineer » certification page, undated, page text consulted 2026-11-08, S588; IEI attributing it to itself). No engineers' title act was found (LOW, unsourced). « Engineer » in a job title is not restricted; do not write « Chartered Engineer » unless the candidate holds it.
- Other councils (NMC, Bar Council) regulate titles by their own acts (LOW, unsourced); not checked here, so check the council before writing a regulated-sounding title.

## Application package
- CTC and notice period: Indian applications and recruiter screening commonly ask for current CTC, expected CTC and notice period (LOW, unsourced; search-result summary only). Keep them out of the CV body; give them in the form, cover email or recruiter call, only if the candidate chooses. Never invent them.
- Government/PSU: recruitment goes through the posting's own application form; do not substitute a CV.
