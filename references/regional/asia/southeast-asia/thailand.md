# Market: Thailand

Loaded on demand when the target market is Thailand. Read together with [`overview.md`](overview.md) (cross-country comparison and shared patterns). Thailand is one of six *non-interchangeable* Southeast Asian markets: never carry a finding from this country to a neighbour.

## At a glance
- Paper: a4 (LOW, unsourced: assumed, not verified in a Thailand-specific source; see Application package).
- Photo: expected, formal studio headshot.
- CV language: Thai (TH); English for multinationals.
- Personal data: customary (including military status) though PDPA-compliance guidance tells employers not to ask.
- Length: 1-2 p. (LOW, unsourced).
- Formality / verb style: formal; the expected-salary field is standard.

## Application package
- Paper: a4 assumed from regional/ISO practice but **not verified in a Thailand-specific source** (LOW, unsourced: label the assumption).
- Photo: a **formal studio headshot is standard and expected**, top-right; dark suit or formal attire, neutral background. Omitting it is noticed. Formal, front-facing, plain background (LOW, unsourced: convention, no source found).
- **Buddhist Era dates (พ.ศ. / B.E. = CE + 543)** appear on Thai-language transcripts, certificates and official forms. Any BE date on a document fed into a foreign-facing CV must be converted to CE (or annotated both ways) — otherwise a graduation year lands 543 years off and an ATS or reader misdates the whole education block. This is the single most important non-obvious convention in this file. (LOW, unsourced)
- Language: Thai for domestic and government employers, English for multinationals and foreign-owned firms. (LOW, unsourced)
- Sections: ประวัติส่วนตัว (personal profile) · จุดมุ่งหมายในงาน / เป้าหมายอาชีพ (objective) · ประวัติการทำงาน · ประวัติการศึกษา · ทักษะและความสามารถพิเศษ · บุคคลที่สามารถติดต่อได้ (references). Real Thai templates put **เงินเดือนต้องการ (expected salary)** inside the personal block as a standard field (LOW, unsourced: from actual local templates).
- Legacy conventions (nickname ชื่อเล่น as a CV field; height/weight/blood type on old-style forms) could not be confirmed as current: do not assert either. Transcript attachment is commonly expected for entry-level, government and university-linked applications (LOW, unsourced).

## Personal data and law
- Personal data: DOB, nationality, religion, marital status and — for male applicants — **military service status** (Thailand conscripts; completion or exemption is commonly stated) are customary on the CV. **But** current PDPA-compliance guidance tells employers not to *request* marital status, religion (sensitive data needing explicit consent) or family plans, to issue a privacy notice, and to delete unsuccessful applicants' data within six months. **Custom (candidate volunteers) and compliance (employer must not ask) genuinely diverge here** — flag it to the user rather than resolving it silently; the candidate decides. (LOW, unsourced)

## Protected titles
Regulated-title claims here are the known case, never the exhaustive list — the universal rule in [`references/regional.md`](../../../regional.md) still applies.
- The **Council of Engineers Thailand (COE / กว.)** under the Engineer Act B.E. 2542 (1999) licenses controlled engineering work: s. 45 forbids anyone to practise controlled engineering, or to represent by any means that they are ready to, in a branch without a COE licence in that branch (Office of the Council of State of Thailand, opinion « เรื่องเสร็จที่ ๘๓๗/๒๕๔๖ », December 2003, footnote 2 quoting the section, consulted 2026-11-08, S562).
- Penalties and the related prohibition: s. 71 punishes a breach of s. 45 with up to three years' imprisonment or a fine up to 60,000 baht, or both; s. 47 bars using words or statements implying expertise in controlled engineering without the required certificate or licence, punished by s. 72 with up to one year or 20,000 baht, or both; maximums as enacted, amendments to ss. 71-72 not checked (Council of Engineers Thailand, « เล่มพรบ.วิศวกร-2542-ปรับปรุง-26-ต.ค.-2566 », compilation of the Act, Royal Gazette vol. 116 part 120 ก, 28 Nov 1999, consulted 2026-11-08, S589; read from the scanned page image, Thai wording cross-checked on a text copy at thaince.org).
- Foreigners are not categorically barred from engineering: a COE regulation of 2023 (Council of Engineers Thailand, « ข้อบังคับสภาวิศวกร ว่าด้วยการขึ้นทะเบียนวิศวกรวิชาชีพต่างชาติ พ.ศ. ๒๕๖๖ » (Regulation on Registration of Foreign Professional Engineers B.E. 2566), Royal Gazette vol. 140 special part 146 ง, 21 June 2023, consulted 2026-11-07, via Wayback, S536) lets foreign engineers registered in a country the COE has announced (art. 3) under a cross-border services agreement register with the COE (arts. 5-7) for cross-border practice, art. 9 sets the duties of registered foreign engineers: comply with Thai law, and practise in collaboration with a local engineer or independently under that agreement; art. 4(1): applicant must not have permanent domicile in Thailand. The architects' position was not verified. Relevant to a foreign candidate's title choice, not just to wording.
- Council of Architects, Medical Council of Thailand, Lawyers Council of Thailand and TFAC (accountants) exist analogously but were not re-verified (LOW, unsourced).

## Thailand specifics
- Credentials to gloss: **Mathayom 6 (M.6)** upper-secondary, or **ปวช. (Por Wor Chor)** vocational certificate, both ≈ high-school completion; **ปวส. (Por Wor Sor)** higher vocational diploma; bachelor's; **GPAX** cumulative GPA on 4.0 (admissions run through TCAS). Registry: **MHESI** (Office of the Permanent Secretary) for recognised institutions and programmes, **ONESQA** for quality-assessment reports.
- Channels: JobsDB and JobThai dominate; LinkedIn is supplementary, networking- and recruiter-facing (LOW, unsourced on the ranking; no penetration figure found).
