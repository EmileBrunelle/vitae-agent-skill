# Japan: official-source facts only

Loaded on demand, in addition to the target market's file, when the CV goes to a Japanese employer (including a foreign applicant moving to Japan), or when the candidate's home habits are Japanese and the target is elsewhere. There is no full market file: only what an official source supports is below; everything else (layout, tone, length) is unverified: ask the user or a current local source, and label the assumption.

## Employer documents (rirekisho, shokumu keirekisho)
- Two documents, not one. Hello Work describes the shokumu keirekisho (職務経歴書) as a free-format document of about 1-2 A4 sheets that details the duties held, separate from the rirekisho (履歴書), which employers generally ask for first and for which they increasingly also ask the shokumu keirekisho (Hello Work pamphlet « 職務経歴書 », labelled PL050531首04, file dated 2023-06; S217). The same pamphlet says to add detail beyond the rirekisho rather than copy it, and to tailor the content to each employer (« 同一の内容を複数の応募先企業に使い回しすることは適当でありません »). A Western-style CV corresponds to the shokumu keirekisho, not the rirekisho (inference from S217; LOW): a rirekisho is a form to fill, not a CV to design.
- The ministry's own rirekisho sample (MHLW 履歴書様式例, undated leaflet « 新たな履歴書の様式例の作成について ») keeps a photo box headed « 写真をはる必要がある場合 » (if a photo is needed: head and shoulders, alone), the date of birth with age (« 年月日生（満 歳） »), a motivation box and a requests box. The gender field is optional (« 任意記載欄 », may be left blank). The fields commute time, number of dependants (excluding spouse), spouse and spousal support obligation were removed as highly private (S218). MHLW presents it as a reference: employers may use another form, but should avoid items that are undesirable for fair selection (S218). Do not add family or commute fields; whether a given employer's own form still asks for them is not covered by the source.
- Not found: any official source on how a non-Japanese candidate should format either document (JETRO and JASSO pages returned showed nothing on it).

## Foreign applicant moving to Japan
- Home history on the forms. The Tokyo Foreign Workforce Employment Service Center (MHLW) tells students to enter official home-country qualifications in the qualification box with the country in parentheses, to write the native language in the skills box (« 語学(北京語、英語) »), and, with education or work history at home, to submit a shokumu keirekisho besides the rirekisho; every entry will be asked about at interview (« 履歴書に記入されている項目は面接で必ず質問されます »). The page is undated (copyright 2011-2012): a dated habit, not a current rule (S219).
- The status of residence reads the CV's facts. For Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services (技術・人文知識・国際業務) the Immigration Services Agency (ISA) asks for a graduation certificate or proof of equivalent education, or for work needing foreign-culture-based thinking or sensitivity a certificate of « 関連する業務について３年以上の実務経験 », unless a graduate works as translator, interpreter or language instructor (S220). JETRO adds a route of more than 10 years of related experience (study time in the subject counts), and pay at least equal to a Japanese in the same job (S221; 10 years single-source). ISA's Q&A adds two limits: a degree-equivalent foreign vocational-school diploma does not meet the education criterion (Japanese 専門士 titles do), and experience as a technical intern or Specified Skilled Worker does not count as 実務経験 (S222, Q35, Q41). The employer-issued employment certificate has no fixed form but lists department, entry date, position, salary and duties (S222, Q18): CV dates and duties should agree with it (inference).
- Language-based jobs. A Japanese-proficiency certificate is required when the job is mainly interpreting/translation or customer service (S222, Q28). For applications from 2026-04-15, employer categories 3 and 4 must add CEFR B2-equivalent proof for such roles, and JLPT N2 or above counts as B2 (S220).
- Highly Skilled Professional points (ISA table, advanced specialized/technical column), only the rows a CV feeds: doctorate 30, master's 20, bachelor's 10, +5 for several advanced degrees or an MBA/MOT; related experience only (Note 1) 3 years 5, 5 years 10, 7 years 15, 10 years 20; Japanese bonus 15 for N1 (or BJT 480) and 10 for N2 (or BJT 400), not cumulative with the Japanese-degree or N1 bonuses; 5 for a foreign qualification related to the work. The N2 value is garbled in the PDF; 10 is the value in the extracted text order, unconfirmed visually (S223).
- JLPT. The organiser words every level as understanding (N1 « The ability to understand Japanese used in a variety of circumstances. »), with no claim about speaking or writing (S224).
- Not found: official apostille or degree-recognition rules for a foreign degree.
