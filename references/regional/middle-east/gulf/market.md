# Market: Gulf (GCC) for expatriate applicants — UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar

Loaded when the CV goes to an employer in the UAE, Saudi Arabia or Qatar and the candidate is not a national (the usual case: these markets hire foreign workers). Only official-source facts, read 2026-11-07: **no source found gives CV custom** (photo, date of birth, nationality, marital status, length, paper, language) for any Gulf state, so none is stated; do not infer it from training data, and do not carry one country's rules to another. Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain are not covered. Companion files: [`gulf/nationalisation.md`](nationalisation.md) (roles closed to or quota-limited for foreigners), [`gulf/credentials.md`](credentials.md) (degree chains, licences).

## Personal data: what the law says, not what custom is
- UAE: the labour law prohibits discrimination « on the basis of race, colour, sex, religion, national or social origin or disability » but says rules « that aim to enhance the participation of UAE citizens in the labour market shall not constitute discrimination » (Federal Decree-Law 33/2021, art. 4; English text on the UAE portal, « NOT an official translation »; S240). Nationality preference is lawful: expect nationality to matter, do not hide it.
- Saudi Arabia: citizens are equal in the right to work « without discrimination on the basis of gender, disability, age, or any other form of discrimination », including « at the time of advertising » (Labor Law 2005, art. 3; S241). It protects *citizens* and says nothing about photos or CV fields.
- Qatar: no source on CV fields or discrimination. For photo, age, marital status in any of the three countries, apply the universal rule in [`regional.md`](../../../regional.md): omit, say the custom is unverified, the candidate decides. Never a passport, residence or national ID number.

## What an expatriate's CV should let the recruiter see (skill guidance, convention, LOW; each item is a fact the candidate supplies)
- **Current country and city** in the header, and **work-authorisation status** in one plain line only if true (« Resident in `<country>`, employer-transferable » / « Based outside the Gulf, available to relocate »). Why: in the UAE a residency visa is mandatory for expatriates other than GCC nationals and working on a visit or tourist visa is prohibited (S242); the signed job offer is attached to the initial work-permit application (S243). Never write a status the candidate does not hold.
- **Notice period** as the contract states it, never a guess. Statutory range: UAE 30 to 90 days in the text read (art. 43; the u.ae page labels that PDF « and Its Amendments » but Decree-Law 20 of 2023, which amends the law, was not read; S240, S244); Qatar one month in the first two years of employment, two months after (Law 18/2020, art. 49; S245); Saudi not sourced.
- **Probation** (UAE, Qatar: at most six months; Saudi: 90 days, extendable by written agreement to 180; S246, S245, S241): a UAE employee switching employer during probation owes the current employer one month's written notice and the new employer may owe recruitment costs (S246). Relevant when the candidate is already in the country and available.
- **Jobseeker route (UAE only):** a 60, 90 or 120-day visit visa without a sponsor, for skill levels 1-3 or a graduate of a top-500 university within the last 2 years, with a bachelor's degree and an attested certificate; no work allowed on it (S247, S242).
- **Degrees and licences:** exact names, issuing body, attestation status (see [`gulf/credentials.md`](credentials.md)). The UAE counts a job as « skilled » only with a professional-level role, an attested post-secondary certificate and at least AED 4,000 a month (S248), so a missing attestation can change the permit category, not just the CV.
- **Language:** no source on CV language. The UAE job offer is issued in Arabic, English and a third language the worker understands (S243): that is the contract, not the CV. Follow the posting's language; list Arabic only at the level the candidate holds.

## Do not
- Do not state or imply reserved-role eligibility the candidate lacks; check [`gulf/nationalisation.md`](nationalisation.md) first.
- Do not write « licensed » / « accredited » / « registered » for engineering or health titles before the step in [`gulf/credentials.md`](credentials.md) is done: write the home-country credential as issued, and a Saudi classification or registration only if true.
