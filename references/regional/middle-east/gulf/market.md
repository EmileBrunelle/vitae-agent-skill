# Market: Gulf (GCC) for expatriate applicants (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar)

Loaded on demand when the CV goes to an employer in the UAE, Saudi Arabia or Qatar and the candidate is not a national (the usual case: these markets hire foreign workers). Read together with [`gulf/nationalisation.md`](nationalisation.md) (roles closed to or quota-limited for foreigners) and [`gulf/credentials.md`](credentials.md) (degree chains, licences), each followed by the target country's file in `nationalisation/` or `credentials/`. Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain are not covered.

## At a glance
- Paper: not covered here (not checked): no source found gives CV custom for any Gulf state; see "Scope" below.
- Photo: not covered here (not checked): no source found gives CV custom for any Gulf state; for photo, age and marital status apply the universal rule in [`regional.md`](../../../regional.md): omit, say the custom was not checked, the candidate decides (see "Personal data: what the law says, not what custom is" below).
- CV language: no source on CV language; the UAE job offer is issued in Arabic, English and a third language the worker understands (UAE Government portal (u.ae), « Job offers and the employment process », 2026-10-14, consulted 2026-11-07, S243): that is the contract, not the CV. Follow the posting's language; list Arabic only at the level the candidate holds.
- Personal data: see "Personal data: what the law says, not what custom is" below; the CV fields are not covered here (not checked): no source found gives CV custom for any Gulf state.
- Length: not covered here (not checked): no source found gives CV custom for any Gulf state; see "Scope" below.

## Scope

Only official-source facts, consulted 2026-11-07: **no source found gives CV custom** (photo, date of birth, nationality, marital status, length, paper, language) for any Gulf state, so none is stated; do not infer it from training data, and do not carry one country's rules to another.

## Personal data: what the law says, not what custom is
- UAE: the labour law prohibits discrimination « on the basis of race, colour, sex, religion, national or social origin or disability » but says rules « that aim to enhance the participation of UAE citizens in the labour market shall not constitute discrimination » (UAE, « Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 », art. 4, undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S240; English text on the UAE portal, « NOT an official translation »). Nationality preference is lawful: expect nationality to matter, do not hide it.
- Saudi Arabia: citizens are equal in the right to work « without discrimination on the basis of gender, disability, age, or any other form of discrimination », including « at the time of advertising » (HRSD, « Labor Law, Royal Decree No. M/51, September 27, 2005 », art. 3, 2023-02, consulted 2026-11-07, S241). It protects *citizens* and says nothing about photos or CV fields.
- Qatar: no source on CV fields or discrimination. For photo, age, marital status in any of the three countries, apply the universal rule in [`regional.md`](../../../regional.md): omit, say the custom was not checked, the candidate decides. Never a passport, residence or national ID number.

## What an expatriate's CV should let the recruiter see (skill guidance, convention, LOW, unsourced; each item is a fact the candidate supplies)
- **Current country and city** in the header, and **work-authorisation status** in one plain line only if true (« Resident in `<country>`, employer-transferable » / « Based outside the Gulf, available to relocate »). Why: in the UAE a residency visa is mandatory for expatriates other than GCC nationals and working on a visit or tourist visa is prohibited (UAE Government portal (u.ae), « Preparing to work », 2026-09-24, consulted 2026-11-07, S242); the signed job offer is attached to the initial work-permit application (UAE Government portal (u.ae), « Job offers and the employment process », 2026-10-14, consulted 2026-11-07, S243). Never write a status the candidate does not hold.
- **Notice period** as the contract states it, never a guess. Statutory range: UAE 30 to 90 days in the text read (art. 43; the u.ae page labels that PDF « and Its Amendments » but Decree-Law 20 of 2023, which amends the law, was not read; UAE, « Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 », undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S240; UAE Government portal (u.ae), « UAE labour law », undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S244); Qatar one month in the first two years of employment, two months after (Qatar, « Law No. 18 of 2020 amending Labour Law 14 of 2004 », art. 49, undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S245); Saudi: no source found.
- **Probation** (UAE, Qatar: at most six months; Saudi: 90 days, extendable by written agreement to 180; UAE Government portal (u.ae), « Employment contracts: duration and models in the private sector », 2026-08-12, consulted 2026-11-07, S246; Qatar, « Law No. 18 of 2020 amending Labour Law 14 of 2004 », undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S245; HRSD, « Labor Law, Royal Decree No. M/51, September 27, 2005 », 2023-02, consulted 2026-11-07, S241): a UAE employee switching employer during probation owes the current employer one month's written notice and the new employer may owe recruitment costs (S246). Relevant when the candidate is already in the country and available.
- **Jobseeker route (UAE only):** a 60, 90 or 120-day visit visa without a sponsor, for skill levels 1-3 or a graduate of a top-500 university within the last 2 years, with a bachelor's degree and an attested certificate; no work allowed on it (UAE Government portal (u.ae), « Jobseeker visit visa », 2026-09-28, consulted 2026-11-07, S247; UAE Government portal (u.ae), « Preparing to work », 2026-09-24, consulted 2026-11-07, S242).
- **Degrees and licences:** exact names, issuing body, attestation status (see [`gulf/credentials.md`](credentials.md)). The UAE counts a job as « skilled » only with a professional-level role, an attested post-secondary certificate and at least AED 4,000 a month (UAE Government portal (u.ae), « Professional levels of jobs in the UAE », 2026-08-12, consulted 2026-11-07, S248), so a missing attestation can change the permit category, not just the CV.
- **Language:** no source on CV language. The UAE job offer is issued in Arabic, English and a third language the worker understands (UAE Government portal (u.ae), « Job offers and the employment process », 2026-10-14, consulted 2026-11-07, S243): that is the contract, not the CV. Follow the posting's language; list Arabic only at the level the candidate holds.

## Do not
- Do not state or imply reserved-role eligibility the candidate lacks; check [`gulf/nationalisation.md`](nationalisation.md) first.
- Do not write « licensed » / « accredited » / « registered » for engineering or health titles before the step in [`gulf/credentials.md`](credentials.md) is done: write the home-country credential as issued, and a Saudi classification or registration only if true.
