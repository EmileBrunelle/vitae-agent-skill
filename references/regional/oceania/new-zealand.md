# Market: New Zealand

Points not confirmed by an official or authoritative source are labelled LOW confidence.

## At a glance
- Paper: a4.
- Photo: no images in the CV: Tahatu Career Navigator (formerly Careers New Zealand) lists « don’t use images » among its CV format tips (S543; a format tip on images, not a statement on photos as such). Omit unless the employer asks (University of Otago Career Development, « CV », n.d.; LOW confidence on that part).
- CV language: English (en-NZ spelling, LOW confidence); « CV » in New Zealand.
- Personal data: omit age, marital status, religion, nationality and date of birth unless the application asks; bank details never (SEEK, « How to write a resume in Australia », updated 29 October 2026).
- Formality / verb style: plain professional English; no market-specific verb rule found.
- Length: Tahatu: « keep your CV to 2 pages if you can » (S543).

## Personal data and law
- New Zealand: Employment New Zealand lists age, race or colour, ethnicity or national origins, sex (including pregnancy or childbirth), sexual orientation, disability, religious or ethical belief, marital or family status, employment status, political opinion, being affected by family violence and union involvement as prohibited grounds (Human Rights Act 1993 and Employment Relations Act 2000); the Human Rights Act applies to most aspects of employment, including job advertisements, application forms, interviews and job offers (S560). Omit them by default.

## Work rights
- A one-line work-rights statement (citizen / permanent resident / visa type and expiry, or « full work rights ») is common practice but no official source requires it (LOW confidence). Include it when the candidate is on a temporary visa or the ad asks; never add a passport or visa number.

## Protected titles
- New Zealand: « Chartered Professional Engineer » (CPEng) is a protected title, and only CPEngs may sign off certain building work (Registration Authority, « What is CPEng? », n.d.). The Chartered Professional Engineers of New Zealand Act 2002 s 7 bars others from using that title or similar words, initials or abbreviations in business or employment (offence, fine up to NZ$5,000, S530); s 7(2) allows overseas-awarded titles to be used in accordance with the rules; it protects the title, not « engineer » alone (other statutes not checked).

## Application package
- New Zealand: most employers ask for two referees who can speak to the candidate's work; « referees available on request » is also accepted; get consent first (LOW confidence; Careers New Zealand, « Tips for creating a NZ-style CV », n.d., archived; University of Otago Career Development, « CV », n.d.).
