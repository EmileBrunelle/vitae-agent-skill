# Market: Australia / New Zealand

Two *non-interchangeable* markets in one file: read the country notes, never the average. Points not confirmed by an official or authoritative source are labelled LOW confidence.

## At a glance
- Paper: a4 (both).
- Photo: omit (Australia: LOW confidence, absence from official guidance). New Zealand: omit unless the employer asks (LOW confidence; University of Otago Career Development, « CV », n.d.).
- CV language: English (en-AU / en-NZ spelling, LOW confidence); « resume » in Australia, « CV » in New Zealand.
- Personal data: omit age, marital status, religion, nationality and date of birth unless the application asks; bank details never (SEEK, « How to write a resume in Australia », updated 29 October 2026).
- Formality / verb style: plain professional English; no market-specific verb rule found.
- Length: Australia: 1 page graduate, 2 typical, 2-3 senior or APS (LOW confidence); follow the ad. New Zealand: 1-2 pages (LOW confidence).

## Personal data and law
- Australia: federal law bars employment discrimination on grounds including age, marital or relationship status, sex, pregnancy, family responsibilities, disability, criminal record, nationality and political opinion; states add more. None of it requires or forbids a CV line; omission is the safe default (SEEK, 2026; Australian Human Rights Commission, « Other areas of workplace discrimination », n.d.; LOW confidence on the list of grounds).
- New Zealand: the Human Rights Act 1993 s 21 lists 13 prohibited grounds (including sex, age, family status, disability, political opinion, employment status, sexual orientation) and applies to adverts, application forms, interviews and offers (LOW confidence on the list; Employment New Zealand, « Discrimination when hiring », n.d.). Omit them by default.

## Work rights
- A one-line work-rights statement (citizen / permanent resident / visa type and expiry, or « full work rights ») is common practice but no official source requires it (LOW confidence). Include it when the candidate is on a temporary visa or the ad asks; never add a passport or visa number.
- Australian Public Service: non-citizens are engaged only where the Agency Head waives the requirement (Public Service Act 1999 s 22(8)) and some agencies require citizenship outright, e.g. DFAT (Australian Public Service Commission, « Citizenship in the APS », n.d.). (LOW confidence.) Check the ad before telling a non-citizen the role is open.

## Protected titles
- « Engineer » alone is not protected in most of Australia: outside the registration regimes « anyone can claim to be an engineer » (Engineers Australia, « Do we need engineering registration? Answering the big questions », 2022). Protected titles are narrower and state-specific.
- Queensland: unregistered persons may not use « registered professional engineer », « RPEQ » (Professional Engineers Act 2002 s 114; BPEQ, « Offences and Investigation Decisions Policy », 2023; LOW confidence on the whole claim).
- Victoria: registration is required for professional engineering services in five prescribed areas (fire safety, civil, structural, electrical, mechanical; chemical/process and software are not prescribed) (Consumer Affairs Victoria, « Who needs to be registered », n.d.); the title « professional engineer » is restricted to registrants under the Professional Engineers Registration Act 2019 (Vic) (LOW confidence on the whole title claim).
- New South Wales: unregistered professional engineering work in prescribed areas is an offence under the Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 (LOW confidence: no source loaded).
- Hold back « RPEQ », « Registered Professional Engineer », « Professional Engineer » (Victoria) and « CPEng » until current registration is confirmed. Engineers Australia « Chartered » status is a voluntary credential, not state registration.
- New Zealand: « Chartered Professional Engineer » (CPEng) is a protected title, and only CPEngs may sign off certain building work (Registration Authority, « What is CPEng? », n.d.). Whether « engineer » alone is protected was not confirmed.

## Application package
- Private sector, Australia: file format as the ad requests; if none is stated, PDF once checked for readability (SEEK, 2026).
- APS and other public-sector jobs: the ad governs. Typical package is a CV plus a one-to-two-page pitch or answers to targeted questions; agencies say addressing every selection criterion separately is no longer required, but some still ask (LOW confidence). CV limits differ by agency.
- New Zealand: most employers ask for two referees who can speak to the candidate's work; « referees available on request » is also accepted; get consent first (LOW confidence; Careers New Zealand, « Tips for creating a NZ-style CV », n.d., archived; University of Otago Career Development, « CV », n.d.).
