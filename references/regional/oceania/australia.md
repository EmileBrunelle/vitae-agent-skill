# Market: Australia

Points not confirmed by an official or authoritative source are labelled LOW confidence.

## At a glance
- Paper: a4.
- Photo: omit (LOW confidence, absence from official guidance).
- CV language: English (en-AU spelling, LOW confidence); « resume » in Australia.
- Personal data: no need to include age, gender or marital status (ask the candidate): « There’s no need to include your age, gender, or marital status » (APSC, S545); name, phone and a professional email are « all you need to provide in the first instance » (DEWR, S544). Religion, nationality and date of birth unless the application asks, and bank details never: SEEK only, not official (SEEK, « How to write a resume in Australia », updated 29 October 2026).
- Formality / verb style: plain professional English; no market-specific verb rule found.
- Length: Workforce Australia (DEWR, « Applying for a job », S544): « no more than 1 to 2 pages », and its checklist « no more than two pages (or no more than 3 pages if referees are included) »; APS applications: « Most CVs are between two and six pages long »; a panel « may request a specific length » (Australian Public Service Commission, « Applying for an APS job: cracking the code », S545). Follow the ad or the agency's limit.

## Personal data and law
- Australia: the Australian Human Rights Commission lists age, criminal record, disability, marital status, nationality, political opinion, religion, sex, sexual orientation and others among the grounds covered by the AHRC Act in employment (S559); other federal Acts add more (e.g. pregnancy, family responsibilities: not verified here); states add more. None of it requires or forbids a CV line; omission is the safe default (SEEK, 2026).

## Work rights
- A one-line work-rights statement (citizen / permanent resident / visa type and expiry, or « full work rights ») is common practice but no official source requires it (LOW confidence). Include it when the candidate is on a temporary visa or the ad asks; never add a passport or visa number.
- Australian Public Service: an Agency Head must not engage a non-citizen « unless the Agency Head considers it appropriate to do so » (Public Service Act 1999 s 22(8), S564); agencies may limit this further (APSC, « Citizenship in the APS », S565); the DFAT Graduate Program lists « Australian citizenship » as an eligibility criterion (S566). Some other agencies may require citizenship outright (LOW confidence). Check the ad before telling a non-citizen the role is open.

## Protected titles
- « Engineer » alone is not protected in most of Australia: outside the registration regimes « anyone can claim to be an engineer » (Engineers Australia, « Do we need engineering registration? Answering the big questions », 2022). Protected titles are narrower and state-specific.
- Queensland: a person who is not a registered professional engineer must not use « registered professional engineer », « registered professional engineer of Queensland » or « RPEQ » (Professional Engineers Act 2002 (Qld) s 114, reprint current as at 1 July 2024, S528). Carrying out professional engineering services is also restricted to practising professional engineers, except under their direct supervision (s 115).
- Victoria: registration is required for professional engineering services in five prescribed areas (fire safety, civil, structural, electrical, mechanical; chemical/process and software are not prescribed) (Consumer Affairs Victoria, « Who needs to be registered », n.d.); the title « professional engineer » is restricted to registrants under the Professional Engineers Registration Act 2019 (Vic) (LOW confidence on the whole title claim).
- New South Wales: carrying out professional engineering work in a prescribed area (structural, civil, mechanical, fire safety, electrical, and areas added by regulation) without being a registered professional engineer, or working under one's direct supervision, is an offence (Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 (NSW) s 32, version in force 2025-02-15, S529); falsely claiming registration is a separate offence (s 58). Sections 32 and 58 create no protected title « engineer »; other NSW statutes not checked.
- Hold back « RPEQ », « Registered Professional Engineer », « Professional Engineer » (Victoria) and « CPEng » until current registration is confirmed. Engineers Australia « Chartered » status is a voluntary credential, not state registration.

## Application package
- Private sector, Australia: file format as the ad requests; if none is stated, PDF once checked for readability (SEEK, 2026).
- APS and other public-sector jobs: the ad governs. Typical package is a CV plus a one-to-two-page pitch or answers to targeted questions; agencies say addressing every selection criterion separately is no longer required, but some still ask (LOW confidence). CV limits differ by agency.
