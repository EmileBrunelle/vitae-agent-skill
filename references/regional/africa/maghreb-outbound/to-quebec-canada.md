# Maghreb credentials, Québec and Canada

Add-on to [`common.md`](common.md) and to the target market's file ([`north-america/quebec/market.md`](../../north-america/quebec/market.md) and [`north-america/quebec/education.md`](../../north-america/quebec/education.md) for Québec, [`north-america/common.md`](../../north-america/common.md) + [`canada/market.md`](../../north-america/canada/market.md) for English Canada). The MIFI evaluation line on the CV (original title, level quoted from the evaluation, never « équivalent ») is in `education.md`; the IRCC ECA basics are in [`europe/to-north-america.md`](../../europe/to-north-america.md); not repeated.

## Which evaluation, for whom
- Three documents, three readers. (1) MIFI « évaluation comparative »: « n'est pas un diplôme ni une équivalence de diplôme », « délivrée à titre indicatif seulement », « n'est pas requise pour présenter une demande d'immigration »; employers, regulatory bodies and schools may use it (quebec.ca, « À propos de l’évaluation comparative », consulted 2026-11-07, S275). (2) IRCC ECA, for Express Entry: from an organization or professional body designated by IRCC, less than 5 years old when the profile is completed and when the application is submitted (IRCC, « Educational credential assessment », consulted 2026-11-07, S122). (3) The licensing body's own evaluation for a regulated profession: OIQ says the MIFI evaluation « ne peut pas remplacer l’évaluation de WES » (OIQ, « Diplômés hors Canada », consulted 2026-11-07, S276).
- The CV carries none of the procedures: the original title and, only if the candidate holds the report, the evaluator's own wording with the evaluator named. Without a report, nothing about level.

## Mixed paths
- IRCC (S122): « You don’t need an assessment for a Canadian degree, diploma or certificate » (earned in Canada: IRCC's score tool says « foreign campuses don’t count » for its Canadian-study question, S279; campus abroad: see last section). For foreign credentials, « in most cases, you only need an assessment for your highest level of education »; to get points for 2 or more credentials « you need an assessment for each one », and for full points at least one must be for 3 or more years of study; the order in which they were earned does not affect points. So an Algerian licence plus a Québec AEC or DEP is a mixed file by design: the foreign diploma is the one an evaluator reports on, the Québec one is simply named as in `education.md`.
- MIFI: « Vous devez déclarer toutes vos formations scolaires depuis l’enseignement primaire, même si vous ne souhaitez pas qu’elles soient évaluées », and each completed post-secondary program needs its diplomas and detailed transcripts for each year (quebec.ca, « Préparer votre demande d’évaluation comparative », consulted 2026-11-07, S277). Inference, not sourced: keep CV dates identical to the transcripts so the CV does not contradict what was declared to the ministry.
- The evaluation can show the language of instruction, on request, from an attestation of « la langue d'enseignement exclusive » (S277); the CV states it only if one exists.
- Résumé advice for skills acquired abroad (quebec.ca, « Having skills acquired abroad recognized by an employer », consulted 2026-11-07, S278): « The names given to jobs may vary from country to country. In your résumé, it is preferable to focus on the description of your tasks and your competencies acquired abroad. » Keep the home job title as printed on the contract, and describe tasks plainly.

## Regulated professions (engineers as the sourced case)
- OIQ (S276): a graduate of a country that signed neither the Washington Accord nor the ARM (France) is a « diplômé d'un pays sans entente »: every university diploma obtained outside Canada needs a WES-ICAP course-by-course report. « l’Ordre regarde d’abord le premier diplôme ou baccalauréat »: the page's example is a bachelor's in a related field followed by an engineering master's, who applies as a non-engineering graduate. Whether a Maghreb program falls under an agreement was not checked: do not assume it.
- During the access program (generally 2 to 5 years, as candidate CPI) the page tells the candidate « vous ne pourrez donc pas exercer seul.e la profession d’ingénieur »; declaring oneself an engineer without the permit risks « usurpation de titre » (S276). Title rules, CPI wording and the « ingénieur d'État » diploma-title question: [`north-america/quebec/market.md`](../../north-america/quebec/market.md#protected-titles).
- Other orders (nurses, lawyers, accountants, architects): own procedures, not loaded here; ask which order and what status. Lawyers and notaries: the title and status rules are in [`north-america/quebec/law.md`](../../north-america/quebec/law.md) (outside Québec: [`north-america/canada/law.md`](../../north-america/canada/law.md)); special authorisation and the temporary restrictive permit for a foreign lawyer are in the Québec file, the full access procedure is not loaded here.

## Campus abroad of a Québec or Canadian school
- Which evaluation applies is officially unresolved. MIFI compares studies « outside Canada » and does not evaluate « Training in a Canadian educational system » (quebec.ca, « About comparative evaluation », last update shown 8 September 2026, S280); IRCC asks an ECA for « education you got outside Canada » (IRCC, ECA page, consulted 2026-11-07, S122). Neither addresses a campus abroad of a Canadian school (nor did any OIQ text found). The CV never says « Canadian credential, no ECA needed » nor the opposite; the candidate asks MIFI, IRCC or the order.
