# Maghreb credentials, other markets: names and mixed paths

Loaded on demand, in addition to the target market's file, when the candidate holds Moroccan, Algerian or Tunisian credentials (alone or mixed with other countries' credentials) and targets Québec/Canada or Europe. Add ONE of [`to-quebec-canada.md`](to-quebec-canada.md) or [`to-europe.md`](to-europe.md). Home-market CV habits: [`../maghreb-common.md`](../maghreb-common.md) and the country file ([`../morocco.md`](../morocco/market.md), [`../algeria.md`](../algeria/market.md), [`../tunisia.md`](../tunisia/market.md)) (not repeated). General cases (transfers, stopped studies, double degrees, grades printed with their scale, never converted): [`references/education-cases.md`](../../../education-cases.md).

## Rules for every credential
- Print the title exactly as on the diploma (French or Arabic form), then institution, city, country, years. Never shorten to « Licence »: Nuffic lists Morocco's Licence d'Études Fondamentales (research-oriented) and Licence Professionnelle as separate diplomas (Nuffic, « Higher education », Morocco, undated, consulted 2026-11-07, S270). Inference from that: the full title tells the reader which one it is.
- Never convert the level in a title the candidate chose: no « = Bac+5 », no « equivalent to a master's »; equivalence is only an evaluator's own wording (see the `to-…` file). Algeria's ministry labels its Master and its ingénieur d'État « Bac+05 » (MESRS Algérie, CNCQ « Bases méthodologiques », December 2024, S271): that is the home label, not a statement about other systems. Nuffic's comparisons to Dutch levels are for Dutch readers: never restate them on a CV.
- A bac mention is not a grade scale: write it as printed.

## Names and lengths (what each ministry or evaluator publishes)
- **Algeria (ministry plus Nuffic).** The ministry: the 2004 reform « a introduit les diplômes standardisés en remplacement des diplômes existants : Licence (3ans), Master (2ans) et le Doctorat de 3ème cycle »; the diplôme d'ingénieur d'État (5 ans) « a été réhabilité … à compter de l'année universitaire 2021-2022 » (S271). Its certification table lists as inactive: licence classique 4 ans, DES 4 ans (and 5 ans), DEUA 3 ans, Technicien Supérieur 3 ans, Magister 6 ans (S271). Nuffic: institutions may still award old-system degrees; « officially all licence study programmes in Algeria last 3 years. They used to last 4 years » (S272). So an Algerian « Licence » can be 3 or 4 years and a DEUA is not a licence: print the years.
- **Morocco.** Names and lengths (Nuffic, S270) are cross-checked against the Moroccan texts in [`morocco/education.md`](../morocco/education.md); a DEUP/DEUG is an intermediate diploma, not a licence.
- **Tunisia.** Names (Nuffic only): Brevet de Technicien Supérieur, Licence Fondamentale, Licence Appliquée, Diplôme National d'Ingénieur, Mastère Professionnel, Mastère de Recherche (Nuffic, « Tunisia », undated, S273). Licence length, from the Official Gazette: décret n° 2008-3123 of 22 September 2008 (JORT n° 79, 30 September 2008, arts. 4-5): the national licence is « appliqué ou fondamental » and lasts « trois ans après le baccalauréat », 180 credits over six semesters; the decree excludes engineering, architecture and medical studies (S274). Amendments since 2008 were not checked. Mastère, doctorat and ingénieur lengths from the Tunisian decrees: [`tunisia/education.md`](../tunisia/education.md).
