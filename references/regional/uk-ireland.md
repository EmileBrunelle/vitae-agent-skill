# Market: United Kingdom / Ireland

Two legal systems, not one market: they share the CV format but differ on equality law, titles and public-sector recruitment. Points that could not be confirmed are labelled LOW confidence.

## At a glance
- Paper: a4 (both).
- Photo: omit (LOW confidence: no official source forbids or requires it; careers-service convention).
- CV language: English, British spelling in both countries (LOW confidence: unsourced convention, no source contradicts it).
- Personal data: no date of birth, age, marital status or nationality (National Careers Service, UK). Ireland: same; age, `civil status` and `family status` are protected grounds.
- Formality / verb style: no sourced market rule; universal rules apply. The word is « CV ».
- Length: UK: no official limit (National Careers Service gives none); two pages is common practice (LOW confidence), one page early-career (LOW confidence). Ireland: Gradireland tells graduates one A4 page, two as an absolute maximum (2023).

## Personal data and law
- UK: the Equality Act 2010 protects nine characteristics (s. 4) and applies in Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own equality legislation (LOW confidence).
- UK: an employer may not ask about health or disability before offering work or placing the applicant in a pool, except narrow cases (Equality Act 2010, s. 60). Do not volunteer health or disability on the CV; the candidate decides on disclosure.
- Ireland: the Employment Equality Acts outlaw discrimination in access to employment, including discriminatory advertisements, on nine grounds: gender, civil status, family status, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability, race, Traveller community (Workplace Relations Commission, « Employment Equality », consulted 2026-11).

## Protected titles
- UK engineering: « engineer » alone is not restricted; no licence is needed to practise (Engineering Council, « FAQs: Professional registration », consulted 2026-11). CEng, IEng, EngTech are protected by civil law under the Royal Charter and usable only by people on the register (UK Government, « Regulated professions: Chartered Engineer », consulted 2026-11).
- Ireland engineering: « Chartered Engineer » (CEng, MIEI) is awarded by Engineers Ireland (LOW confidence on the statutory basis: search snippets only). A UK CEng is not the Irish title; keep the awarding body in the entry.
- UK accountancy: « Chartered Accountant », ACA, FCA (ICAEW), CA (ICAS) and the Chartered Accountants Ireland equivalents are protected titles, with no reserved activity attached (UK Government, « Regulated professions: Chartered Accountant », consulted 2026-11). « Accountant » alone is not restricted in UK law (LOW confidence).
- Ireland accountancy: « accountant » alone is not yet protected; the Companies (Protection of Title: Accountant) Bill 2025 passed to Second Stage (Chartered Accountants Ireland, « Introduction of the Companies (Protection of Title: Accountant) Bill 2025 in Dáil Éireann », 24 October 2025). Re-check status.
- Law (England and Wales): « solicitor » is protected by criminal offence for non-solicitors (Solicitors Act 1974, s. 21) (UK Government, « Regulated professions: Solicitor », consulted 2026-11).

## Degree names and grading
- UK honours: First (70% and above), 2:1 (60-70%), 2:2 (50-60%), Third (40-50%); taught master's: Distinction, Merit, Pass (Prospects, « Understanding university grades in the UK », September 2026). Write the classification as awarded (« First-Class Honours », « 2:1 »).
- Ireland: honours bachelor's is NFQ Level 8; never convert a GPA or mark to a class unless the transcript states it (LOW confidence: institutional thresholds vary). Write the full degree title, not the abbreviation (Gradireland, « The graduate's guide to CVs », 2023).

## Application package
- References: the National Careers Service tells UK candidates not to list referees' contact details and to say references are available on request; Gradireland tells Irish graduates to name usually one academic and one employer or personal referee, with permission (consulted 2026-11; 2023). The two point in different directions; follow the posting.
- Irish public service (publicjobs.ie): an online application form is the basis; a CV is needed only if the competition booklet says so, « usually only for very senior roles or specific technical positions »; a submitted application cannot be amended (Public Appointments Service, « Application advice », consulted 2026-11). Evidence each criterion in the form, not the CV.
- UK public service: Success Profiles frame civil-service applications; the Scottish Government asks for a CV and a Supporting Statement evidencing the criteria (Scottish Government, « Success profiles: candidate guide », 23 June 2026). Other departments and the NHS vary (LOW confidence): follow the advert.
