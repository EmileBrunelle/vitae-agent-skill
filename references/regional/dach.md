# Market: Germany / Austria / Switzerland (DACH)

Three markets sharing a language and a tabular « Lebenslauf » but differing on law, titles and certificates: read the country notes, never the average. Weakly sourced points are labelled LOW confidence.

## At a glance
- Paper: a4 in all three.
- Photo: optional everywhere, no legal duty. Germany: the Bundesagentur describes a photo-free variant for employers who ask for anonymised CVs (Bundesagentur für Arbeit, « Bewerbung schreiben », n.d.). Austria: « Ein Foto ist nicht verpflichtend » (AMS, « Bewerbungsmappe », 2025); still usual in customer-facing fields, less in technical/IT (karriere.at, « Foto im Lebenslauf: Pflicht oder nicht? », 2025). Switzerland: add one only if the ad asks (SECO, « Wie bewerbe ich mich richtig? Bewerbungsdossier », 2018). Follow the posting; tell the user the custom.
- CV language: the posting's language (German; French or Italian in Romandie and Ticino).
- Personal data: omit by default in Germany and Austria (LOW confidence: custom, no source cited here); Switzerland differs for foreigners (below).
- Formality / verb style: tabular, reverse chronological (Bundesagentur; AMS). No official source on verb style: assert none.
- Length: max 2 A4 pages in Germany and Austria (Bundesagentur; AMS, 2025). Switzerland: no official rule found (LOW confidence: 2 pages).

## Personal data and law
- Germany: the AGG says nothing about photos (AGG § 1 lists the grounds); optionality comes from the Bundesagentur's guidance, not from the statute.
- Austria: an application without photo may not be treated as incomplete (karriere.at, 2025; secondary reading of the Gleichbehandlungsgesetz).
- Switzerland: the federal brochure lists « Staatsangehörigkeit » and « Aufenthaltsbewilligung » (bei Ausländern) among personal details, and its sample also shows birth date, Heimatort and Zivilstand (SECO, 2018, a dated sample): Swiss custom, the candidate decides. Never invent a permit or work-authorisation status. Non-EU/EFTA hires require the employer to prove no suitable candidate in Switzerland or the EU/EFTA (SEM, « Nicht-EU/EFTA-Angehörige », 2026); EU/EFTA: permit L under 12 months, B from 12 months, G for cross-border commuters (SEM, « FAQ – Free Movement of Persons », n.d.).

## Protected titles
- Germany: « Ingenieur » is protected by Land law, not federal. NRW: graduates of a technical or natural-science programme of at least 3 years, or authorised persons; misuse is an Ordnungswidrigkeit (Land NRW, « Ingenieurgesetz – IngG », §§ 1, 8). Other Länder differ; a foreign degree needs the Land's check (LOW confidence on the procedure).
- Austria: « Ing. » is a Qualifikationsbezeichnung under the Ingenieurgesetz 2017, not a degree: HTL-type qualification, at least 3 years of practice, a Fachgespräch; written before the name (BMLUK, « Antrag zur Führung des Ingenieurtitels », n.d.). Never give « Ing. » to a candidate with only a foreign bachelor's.
- Austrian degrees: « Mag. », « Dipl.-Ing. »/« DI », « DI (FH) », « Dr. med. univ. » (oesterreich.gv.at, « Diplomgrade », n.d.). Never convert a foreign degree into a Mag. or DI.
- Switzerland: no authoritative source on title protection found (LOW confidence): do not carry over the German or Austrian rules.

## Application package
- Cover letter: Germany, max one A4 page, no conditional (« würde », « könnte »), salary only if asked (Bundesagentur). Austria: mandatory, max one page, in the email body for digital applications, not in the PDF (AMS, 2025).
- Attachments: Germany: certificates and Arbeitszeugnisse of each former employer, scans only, never originals by post (Bundesagentur). Austria: Dienstzeugnisse of the last three employers, highest school certificate; scans « max. 300 kB je Seite » (AMS, « Zeugnisse », 2024). Switzerland: Begleitbrief, CV, copies of all Arbeitszeugnisse and diplomas (SECO, 2018); references only if the ad asks.
- Austria: Deckblatt, Profilblatt and Motivationsschreiben are optional; Bewerbungsmappe order: cover letter on top, CV, certificates (AMS, 2025).

## Zeugnisse (employer certificates)
- Germany: § 109 GewO: on request, a « qualifiziertes Zeugnis » covers performance and conduct on top of type and duration, with no wording meant to convey a hidden meaning.
- Switzerland: Art. 330a OR: « Vollzeugnis » (performance and conduct) or « Arbeitsbestätigung » (type and duration only); truthful, no coded language (SECO, « FAQ zu Arbeitszeugnis », n.d.).
- Austria: « Dienstzeugnis » « darf nichts Negatives enthalten » (AMS notes Austrian Zeugnisse use coded phrasing); the AMS advises not sending a bad one (AMS, « Zeugnisse », 2024).
- A Zeugnis is evidence to attach, never to paraphrase on the CV; never write the codes.
- Switzerland: « ss », never « ß » (LOW confidence); « Heimatort » is a Swiss-only field.
