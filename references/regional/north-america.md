## Protected titles
- "Engineer" / « ingénieur » and designations ("P.Eng.", « ing. ») are reserved by provincial engineering acts in ALL provinces — OIQ (Québec), PEO (Ontario), EGBC (BC), APEGA (Alberta), etc. Degree names are always legal (B.Eng., B. Ing.).
- "Software engineer" as a job title: enforcement varies by province, mostly regulator cease-and-desist letters (e.g. a publicized 2024 case), rarely court-tested. Safest default: "software developer" (« développeur logiciel » in French) unless the employer's own posting uses the engineer title and the role is in the USA (see the USA line below). In Canada the title requires a licence regardless of the employer: the 12 Canadian engineering regulators restrict "software engineer" and similar IT titles to licensed engineers (Engineers Canada, « Engineering regulators reiterate licensure requirements for those using “software engineer” and other IT titles » (2022), consulted 2026-11), and the OIQ says an employer-given title is still an offence.
- USA: the bare word "engineer" is regulated by state, not uniformly (e.g. Texas restricts it; Texas Board of Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors, « PAO-65 response » (2024), consulted 2026-11). Don't claim a rule for a state without a source; flag it. "PE" needs a state licence.
- Foreign engineering degree (European etc.): see [`regional/europe-to-north-america.md`](europe-to-north-america.md).
