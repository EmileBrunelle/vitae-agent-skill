# Market: Québec (Canada)

Add-on to [`north-america/us-canada.md`](../us-canada.md), which still applies; Québec-specific rules only.

## Length and verbs
- Québec French: infinitive action verbs (« Concevoir… », « Livrer… ») — a convention taught by university career services, not a provincial norm; noun-phrase style is equally accepted — don't present it to the user as "the Québec convention". Months lowercase (janv., août); « Juin 2026 – présent », never « Depuis juin 2026 » (not ATS-parseable).
- Québec academic calendars (for bounding year-only education dates, with user confirmation): fall session — cégep starts late August, university early September; winter session — university starts early January, cégep late January; both end in May.

## Protected titles
- Québec reserves professional titles by law. Code des professions, art. 32 (exclusive-practice professions such as ingénieur, architecte) and art. 36 (reserved-title professions): no one may use the title, or "un titre ou une abréviation pouvant laisser croire" they hold it, without the order's permit. Fines start at 2 500 $ for an individual (art. 188); an employer who designates a non-member that way also commits an offence (art. 188.1). Sources: Code des professions, RLRQ c. C-26
- Engineering: the Loi sur les ingénieurs (art. 22) forbids "le titre d'ingénieur seul ou avec qualificatifs". The OIQ names « ingénieur logiciel », « ingénieur qualité », « ingénieur de produit » as offences even when the employer gave the title, and suggests an unambiguous function title instead. OIQ, « Mais c’est mon employeur qui m’a donné ce titre ! », consulted 2026-11
- English titles: the 12 Canadian engineering regulators jointly state that "software engineer", "computer engineer" and similar IT titles are restricted to licensed engineers (2022). Engineers Canada, « Engineering regulators reiterate licensure requirements for those using “software engineer” and other IT titles » (2022), consulted 2026-11
- Rule for the skill: never write, translate or "upgrade" a job title into a reserved one (ingénieur / engineer, architecte, CPA…) unless the candidate confirms membership. If the candidate's real past title contains one and they are not a member, keep the facts but flag it to the candidate and propose a function title (« Développeur logiciel », « Chargé de projets »); never silently rewrite an employer's title.
- « Architecte logiciel / solutions architect »: the Loi sur les architectes (art. 14) forbids any title "susceptible de laisser croire" one practises architecture; no official source settles IT architect titles — flag it, don't rule on it. Source: Loi sur les architectes, RLRQ c. A-21
- The official list of the 46 regulated professions (it does not say which are reserved-title only): Gouvernement du Québec, « Liste des professions et répertoire des membres », consulted 2026-11 — link to it, don't copy it.
- A bachelor's in engineering does not confer the title « ingénieur » / "engineer": the OIQ permit does (Loi sur les ingénieurs, art. 22, above). A graduate who is not a member writes neither, but may write « CPI » if registered.
- A registered candidate may write « candidat à la profession d'ingénieur (CPI) »; same logic for other orders. Source: OIQ, « CPI : avant de s’appeler ingénieur.e », consulted 2026-11
- Health and financial-services titles and registrations (OIIQ, OPQ, CMQ, AMF): [`health.md`](health.md), [`financial-licensing.md`](financial-licensing.md).
- Voluntary designations are not reserved titles: CIPS's I.S.P. is legislated in six provinces but not in Québec (CIPS, « CIPS Provincial Societies », consulted 2026-11) — list it under certifications if held.

## ATS notes
- **Québec DEC / DEP** survive better from a French CV (`DEC, Informatique de Gestion`): this is about keyword matching, not abbreviation style. English-CV renderings: [`north-america/quebec/outbound-common.md`](outbound-common.md).

## Diploma names and abbreviations (OQLF)
- Default to full diploma names: « Évitez les sigles… BACC OU DÉC. Écrivez plutôt Baccalauréat ou Diplôme d'études collégiales. » (UQAM guide, « Le CV et la lettre de présentation », consulted 2026-11). The OQLF forms below are acceptable when space is tight; never « BACC » / « DÉC ». In running text, diploma names are written out in lowercase (« un baccalauréat en informatique »). OQLF generic abbreviations: B., M., D., C., L., dipl.; points and a (non-breaking) space between elements, with accents: B. Sc., M. Sc., B. Éd., B. Ing., M. Ing.; sigles without spaces or accents: DEC (or D.E.C.), DEP, AEC, DES; Latin forms: LL. B., Ph. D., M.D.; M.B.A. or MBA admitted. Institution names are proper nouns (« Université Laval »). Source: OQLF, Banque de dépannage linguistique, « Abréviation des noms de diplômes et de grades universitaires » (2018), consulted 2026-11.
