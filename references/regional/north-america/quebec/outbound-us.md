# Outbound: Québec credentials, USA

Loaded on demand when a candidate with Québec credentials targets the USA. Read together with [`outbound-common.md`](outbound-common.md), which still applies.

- A Canadian licence is not a US PE licence (state boards license; NCEES, « Licensure », consulted 2026-11-07, S20).
- USA, bare word "engineer": see "Protected titles" in [`north-america/us/market.md`](../us/market.md).
- Engineers targeting the USA: list the Canadian licence as held (« ing. (Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec) » / "P.Eng. (PEO)") and, if the candidate confirms it, the program's CEAB accreditation on the degree line — US boards differ: Texas deems a verified Canadian P.Eng./ing. licence "substantially equivalent to an accredited engineering program" (Cornell LII, « 22 Tex. Admin. Code § 133.31 - Educational Requirements for Applicants », § 133.31(c), consulted 2026-11-07, S43, amended 2025); Florida treats CEAB programs accredited 1980 or later as equivalent to EAC/ABET (Florida Administrative Code, « Rule 61G15-20.001, Fla. Admin. Code », 61G15-20.001(2)(b), consulted 2026-11-07, S144, amended 2019); California states it has no reciprocity agreement with any state or country (California BPELSG, « Application and Licensing FAQs », consulted 2026-11-07, S145).
- California, title trap: a literal translation of a Québec branch title ("ingénieur industriel" → "Industrial Engineer", "ingénieur en contrôle" → "Control System Engineer", civil, électrique, mécanique, chimique…) is a title §6732 restricts to California licensees (California BPELSG, « Professional Engineers Act (Bus. & Prof. Code §§6700-6799) », consulted 2026-11-08, S592; list in [`us/market.md`](../us/market.md)). Write the licence as held (« ing. (OIQ) ») with a non-restricted descriptive title, or the field ("industrial engineering"), never the bare restricted title; "write the field" is a convention (LOW, unsourced).
- California, path to a licence (California BPELSG, « Application and Licensing FAQs », consulted 2026-11-07, S145; California BPELSG, « Professional Engineers Act (Bus. & Prof. Code §§6700-6799) », consulted 2026-11-08, S592; California BPELSG, « Board Rules and Regulations, Cal. Code Regs. tit. 16, div. 5, §§400-476 », consulted 2026-11-08, S593; California BPELSG, « Applying for licensure as a Civil Engineer », consulted 2026-11-08, S594): no reciprocity, and a licensee from another jurisdiction must "meet all the same California requirements" (FAQ Q29). 6 years of qualifying experience, of which an ABET-accredited degree counts 4 years and a non-ABET degree up to 2 (§6751(c)(2), §6753, 16 CCR §§404, 424); no source read mentions CEAB (absence in the sources read, not checked elsewhere). Credential evaluations are not accepted, original transcripts are required (FAQ Q33); experience outside the USA may be considered (§6751.2). The NCEES FE must be passed or waived (among other routes, a waiver at 15 years of added experience with an ABET degree or 17 without, 16 CCR §438(a)(3)-(4)); civil also requires the state Seismic Principles and Engineering Surveying exams. SSN or ITIN is mandatory; no citizenship or residency requirement (FAQ Q1, Q2).
- CV wording for steps in progress, a convention (LOW, unsourced): "FE exam passed (NCEES), <year>" only if passed; "EIT (California)" only if the certificate is held; never "PE" or "PE candidate". Never write "PE" / "Professional Engineer" without a US state licence.
- US credential evaluation (WES, ECE) depends on evaluator and program; ECE: "Not all international degrees are automatically considered equivalent to a U.S. bachelor's degree" (ECE, « How to Get Your Bachelor’s Degree Recognized in the U.S. », consulted 2026-11-07, S22). Never promise the outcome.
- Work authorization: Canadian citizens can work in the USA under TN status for listed professions (Cornell LII, 8 CFR § 214.6, « Citizens of Canada or Mexico seeking temporary entry under USMCA… », consulted 2026-11-07, S23). Engineer: bachelor's or provincial licence, and the degree must be in the related engineering field (USCIS, Policy Manual, Volume 2, Part P, Chapter 6, « Requirements for Specific Occupations », consulted 2026-11-07, S166). Computer Systems Analyst also accepts "Post-Secondary Diploma or Post-Secondary Certificate, and three years experience" (8 CFR 214.6, Appendix 2 to Annex 16-A). No loaded CBP/USCIS text addresses 3-year degrees. H-1B asks for a foreign degree "determined to be equivalent to a United States baccalaureate" (Cornell LII, 8 CFR § 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(C)(2), consulted 2026-11-07, S120), established by a credential evaluation ((D)(3)); no loaded official source rules on a 90-credit Québec bachelor's, so a master's is not required by the text, but equivalence is decided case by case; for the experience route see [`europe/to-north-america.md`](../../europe/to-north-america.md). Whether to state work authorization on the résumé: see "At a glance" in [`north-america/common.md`](../common.md).

## Degree length: identify the path first
- Same three paths and lines as [`outbound-canada.md`](outbound-canada.md#degree-length-identify-the-path-first); read it from the CV, ask only if absent; never assume. Never print Québec credit counts or "3-year" alone: credit units differ by system (a Québec credit is 45 hours at Laval (Université Laval, « Règlement des études », consulted 2026-11-08, S493), UQAM (UQAM, « Règlement no 5, Règlement des études de premier cycle », consulted 2026-11-07, S129), HEC Montréal (HEC Montréal, « Règlement pédagogique », consulted 2026-11-08, S495), Université de Montréal (Université de Montréal, Secrétariat général, « Règlement des études de premier cycle », consulted 2026-11-08, S505), normally 45 at Sherbrooke (Université de Sherbrooke, « Règlement des études, 1. Introduction », consulted 2026-11-08, S476)) and no official US conversion was found.
- A, only when the posting or form requires four years: `3-year program following the 2-year pre-university DEC (CEGEP); 16 years of study, as for a four-year degree after Grade 12` (Concordia University, « Program length », consulted 2026-11-08, S480; CICIC (Gov. of Canada), « Postsecondary education in Quebec », consulted 2026-11-08, S481). Never "equivalent to a four-year degree". B: own DEC line, no total-years or equivalence claim (LOW, unsourced); DEC facts (Gouvernement du Québec, « Education system », consulted 2026-11-08, S485; Université Laval, « Entente DEC-BAC: Baccalauréat en informatique / 420.B0 Techniques de l'informatique, Cégep Édouard-Montpetit », consulted 2026-11-08, S488). C: degree name only (UQAM, « Bases d'admissibilité 1er cycle », consulted 2026-11-08, S475; Concordia University, « Admission requirements: Canada », consulted 2026-11-08, S489).
- Form question "four-year degree?": A: comment field or "equivalent" option first, "Yes" only when nothing else is offered and the arithmetic line is on the CV; B: "equivalent/other" or comment; C: never "Yes". "Bachelor's degree?" without "four-year": "Yes" is true for all three. Convention (LOW, unsourced).
- GPA: scale printed, never converted; see [`education.md`](education.md#gpa-moyenne-cumulative).
- Evaluation: state an evaluator equivalence only if the candidate holds an official report in their name (report's own words, evaluator named); otherwise facts only: degree, institution, years, credits. WES says an equivalency may go in the résumé's education section (WES, « Using Your WES Credential Evaluation to Advance Your Job Search », consulted 2026-11-08, S492). Never promise the result (see ECE above).
- DES or DEP in truck driving: same lines as [`outbound-canada.md`](outbound-canada.md#d-highest-credential-is-not-a-bachelors); licence part: [`../driving.md`](../driving.md#canadian-and-québec-licences). Never call a DES a "US high school diploma" or a SAAQ class 1 a "CDL" (rule).
- Highest credential not a bachelor's (technical or pre-university DEC, DEP, AEC, university certificate, stopped studies): lines and form answers in [`outbound-canada.md`](outbound-canada.md#d-highest-credential-is-not-a-bachelors) apply unchanged. US addition: "associate degree" is a US credential name; never use it for a DEC (no source of correspondence; ECE's caution above applies). Knockout "bachelor's required": "No", truthfully. TN: the Computer Systems Analyst entry above accepts a post-secondary diploma or certificate plus three years of experience; the candidate's own status is not for the résumé to assert.
