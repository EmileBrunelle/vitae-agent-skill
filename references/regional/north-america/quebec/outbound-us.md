# Québec credentials, USA

Add-on to [`outbound-common.md`](outbound-common.md), which still applies.

- A Canadian licence is not a US PE licence (state boards license; NCEES, « Licensure », consulted 2026-11).
- USA, bare word "engineer": see "Protected titles" in [`north-america/us/market.md`](../us/market.md).
- Engineers targeting the USA: list the Canadian licence as held (« ing. (Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec) » / "P.Eng. (PEO)") and, if the candidate confirms it, the program's CEAB accreditation on the degree line — US boards differ: Texas deems a verified Canadian P.Eng./ing. licence "substantially equivalent to an accredited engineering program" (22 Tex. Admin. Code §133.31(c), amended 2025); Florida treats CEAB programs accredited 1980 or later as equivalent to EAC/ABET (Fla. Admin. Code 61G15-20.001(2)(b), amended 2019); California states it has no reciprocity agreement with any state or country (BPELSG, « Application and Licensing FAQs » S145); all consulted 2026-11.
- California, title trap: a literal translation of a Québec branch title ("ingénieur industriel" → "Industrial Engineer", "ingénieur en contrôle" → "Control System Engineer", civil, électrique, mécanique, chimique…) is a title §6732 restricts to California licensees (S592; list in [`us/market.md`](../us/market.md)). Write the licence as held (« ing. (OIQ) ») with a non-restricted descriptive title, or the field ("industrial engineering"), never the bare restricted title; "write the field" is a convention (LOW).
- California, path to a licence (S145, S592, S593, S594): no reciprocity, and a licensee from another jurisdiction must "meet all the same California requirements" (FAQ Q29). 6 years of qualifying experience, of which an ABET-accredited degree counts 4 years and a non-ABET degree up to 2 (§6751(c)(2), §6753, 16 CCR §§404, 424); no source read mentions CEAB (absence, LOW). Credential evaluations are not accepted, original transcripts are required (FAQ Q33); experience outside the USA may be considered (§6751.2). The NCEES FE must be passed or waived (among other routes, a waiver at 15 years of added experience with an ABET degree or 17 without, 16 CCR §438(a)(3)-(4)); civil also requires the state Seismic Principles and Engineering Surveying exams. SSN or ITIN is mandatory; no citizenship or residency requirement (FAQ Q1, Q2).
- CV wording for steps in progress, a convention (LOW): "FE exam passed (NCEES), <year>" only if passed; "EIT (California)" only if the certificate is held; never "PE" or "PE candidate". Never write "PE" / "Professional Engineer" without a US state licence.
- US credential evaluation (WES, ECE) depends on evaluator and program; ECE: "Not all international degrees are automatically considered equivalent to a U.S. bachelor's degree" (ECE, « How to Get Your Bachelor’s Degree Recognized in the U.S. », consulted 2026-11). Never promise the outcome.
- Work authorization: Canadian citizens can work in the USA under TN status for listed professions (8 CFR 214.6, « Citizens of Canada or Mexico seeking temporary entry under USMCA… », consulted 2026-11). Engineer: bachelor's or provincial licence, and the degree must be in the related engineering field (USCIS Policy Manual, vol. 2, part P, ch. 6, « Requirements for Specific Occupations »). Computer Systems Analyst also accepts "Post-Secondary Diploma or Post-Secondary Certificate, and three years experience" (8 CFR 214.6, Appendix 2 to Annex 16-A). No loaded CBP/USCIS text addresses 3-year degrees. H-1B asks for a foreign degree "determined to be equivalent to a United States baccalaureate" (8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(C)(2)), established by a credential evaluation ((D)(3)); no loaded official source rules on a 90-credit Québec bachelor's, so a master's is not required by the text, but equivalence is decided case by case; for the experience route see [`europe/to-north-america.md`](../../europe/to-north-america.md). Whether to state work authorization on the résumé: see "At a glance" in [`north-america/common.md`](../common.md).

## Degree length: identify the path first
- Same three paths and lines as [`outbound-canada.md`](outbound-canada.md#degree-length-identify-the-path-first); read it from the CV, ask only if absent; never assume. Never print Québec credit counts or "3-year" alone: credit units differ by system (a Québec credit is 45 hours at Laval S493, UQAM S129, HEC Montréal S495, Université de Montréal S505, normally 45 at Sherbrooke S476) and no official US conversion was found.
- A, only when the posting or form requires four years: `3-year program following the 2-year pre-university DEC (CEGEP); 16 years of study, as for a four-year degree after Grade 12` S480, S481 (HIGH arithmetic). Never "equivalent to a four-year degree". B: own DEC line, no total-years or equivalence claim (unsourced) S485, S488. C: degree name only S475, S489.
- Form question "four-year degree?": A: comment field or "equivalent" option first, "Yes" only when nothing else is offered and the arithmetic line is on the CV; B: "equivalent/other" or comment; C: never "Yes". "Bachelor's degree?" without "four-year": "Yes" is true for all three. Convention (LOW).
- GPA: scale printed, never converted; see [`education.md`](education.md#gpa-moyenne-cumulative).
- Evaluation: state an evaluator equivalence only if the candidate holds an official report in their name (report's own words, evaluator named); otherwise facts only: degree, institution, years, credits. WES says an equivalency may go in the résumé's education section S492. Never promise the result (see ECE above).
- DES or DEP in truck driving: same lines as [`outbound-canada.md`](outbound-canada.md#d-highest-credential-is-not-a-bachelors); licence part: [`../driving.md`](../driving.md#canadian-and-québec-licences). Never call a DES a "US high school diploma" or a SAAQ class 1 a "CDL" (rule).
- Highest credential not a bachelor's (technical or pre-university DEC, DEP, AEC, university certificate, stopped studies): lines and form answers in [`outbound-canada.md`](outbound-canada.md#d-highest-credential-is-not-a-bachelors) apply unchanged. US addition: "associate degree" is a US credential name; never use it for a DEC (no source of correspondence; ECE's caution above applies). Knockout "bachelor's required": "No", truthfully. TN: the Computer Systems Analyst entry above accepts a post-secondary diploma or certificate plus three years of experience; the candidate's own status is not for the résumé to assert.
