# Market: Canada / Québec / USA

## At a glance
- Paper: us-letter. Photo: never.
- CV language: English, or French for Québec and francophone employers elsewhere in Canada.
- Personal data: none (no age, no marital status). USA: the EEOC says employers "should not ask for a photograph", and age (40 or older) and national origin are protected (EEOC, « Prohibited Employment Policies/Practices », consulted 2026-11); leaving out photo, date of birth and nationality is therefore the convention (LOW confidence for the convention part).
- Work authorization: no official candidate-side guidance exists (LOW confidence: convention). State it only if the posting asks or the candidate wants it; never invent a status.
- Formality / verb style: verb-first bullets; English past tense (current role in present); French-Québec infinitive (see [`north-america/quebec/market.md`](quebec/market.md)).
- Length: 1 page junior, 2 pages max overall (US established firms expect 1 page well past 10 years).

## Protected titles
- "Engineer" / « ingénieur » and designations ("P.Eng.", « ing. ») are reserved by provincial engineering acts in ALL provinces — OIQ (Québec), PEO (Ontario), EGBC (BC), APEGA (Alberta), etc. Degree names are always legal (B.Eng., B. Ing.).
- "Software engineer" as a job title: enforcement varies by province, mostly regulator cease-and-desist letters, rarely court-tested. Safest default: "software developer" (title in the CV's language) unless the employer's own posting uses the engineer title and the role is in the USA (see the USA line below). In Canada the title requires a licence regardless of the employer: the 12 Canadian engineering regulators restrict "software engineer" and similar IT titles to licensed engineers (Engineers Canada, « Engineering regulators reiterate licensure requirements for those using “software engineer” and other IT titles » (2022), consulted 2026-11), and the OIQ says an employer-given title is still an offence.
- USA: engineering regulation is state by state. NCEES's Model Law and Model Rules define the education requirement as an EAC/ABET degree or an equivalent meeting the NCEES Engineering Education Standard, and leave a foreign licensee's comity to each board's judgment (NCEES, « Model Law » (2018); « Model Rules » (2016), consulted 2026-11). Titles vary: Florida bars unlicensed persons from titles tending to indicate an active licence, explicitly listing "software engineer", "computer hardware engineer" and "systems engineer" (Fla. Stat. §471.031(1)(b)1, 2026); Texas restricts the word too (TBPELS, « PAO-65 response » (2024)); New York allows only licensed persons to practice engineering "or use the title 'professional engineer'" (N.Y. Educ. Law §7202). Rule: never claim a US licence or a licence-implying title; for a specific state, check its board; don't claim a rule for an unchecked state. "PE" needs a state licence.
- Any regulated occupation (licence required, or titles reserved "where only members of a regulatory body can make use of specific titles and abbreviations allowed by law") exists in every province and state (IRCC, « Regulated or certified occupations – Processing of work permit applications » (2024-05-21); Québec.ca, « Ordres professionnels » (2026-09-29); consulted 2026-11). Before writing a licence-type title, check the regulator (Canada: Job Bank Foreign Credential Recognition tool or the regulatory body; Québec: the order's répertoire des membres; USA: the state licensing board). Write the licence with its jurisdiction and status.
- Health and financial-services licences: [`quebec/health.md`](quebec/health.md), [`health-canada.md`](health-canada.md), [`health-us.md`](health-us.md); [`quebec/financial-licensing.md`](quebec/financial-licensing.md), [`financial-licensing-canada.md`](financial-licensing-canada.md), [`financial-licensing-us.md`](financial-licensing-us.md).
- Foreign engineering degree (European etc.): see [`regional/europe/to-north-america.md`](../europe/to-north-america.md).
