# Market: USA

Add-on to [`common.md`](../common.md), which still applies.

## At a glance
- Personal data: USA: the EEOC says employers "should not ask for a photograph", and age (40 or older) and national origin are protected (EEOC, « Prohibited Employment Policies/Practices », consulted 2026-11); leaving out photo, date of birth and nationality is therefore the convention (LOW confidence for the convention part).

## Protected titles
- USA: engineering regulation is state by state. NCEES's Model Law and Model Rules define the education requirement as an EAC/ABET degree or an equivalent meeting the NCEES Engineering Education Standard, and leave a foreign licensee's comity to each board's judgment (NCEES, « Model Law » (2018); « Model Rules » (2016), consulted 2026-11). Titles vary: Florida bars unlicensed persons from titles tending to indicate an active licence, explicitly listing "software engineer", "computer hardware engineer" and "systems engineer" (Fla. Stat. §471.031(1)(b)1, 2026); Texas restricts the word too (TBPELS, « PAO-65 response » (2024)); New York allows only licensed persons to practice engineering "or use the title 'professional engineer'" (N.Y. Educ. Law §7202). Rule: never claim a US licence or a licence-implying title; for a specific state, check its board; don't claim a rule for an unchecked state. "PE" needs a state licence. California: B&P Code §§6704 and 6732 reserve "professional engineer", "licensed engineer", "registered engineer", "consulting engineer" and the branch titles "agricultural", "chemical", "civil", "control system", "electrical", "fire protection", "industrial", "mechanical", "metallurgical", "nuclear", "petroleum" and "traffic engineer" to licensees, §§6736 and 6736.1 add "structural engineer" and "soil/soils/geotechnical engineer", and §6787(g) and (i) make any other title or abbreviation that suggests licensure a misdemeanor (BPELSG, « Professional Engineers Act » (2026), S592). "Software engineer" and "systems engineer" are not in that list and BPELSG's FAQ lists no software branch (S145, Q18): not listed, but no source says they are allowed and §6787(i) is a catch-all (LOW). §6747 exempts industrial-corporation employees from practice rules, not from titles (§§6740, 6746.1 keep the §6732 titles restricted for the employees they cover) (S592).
