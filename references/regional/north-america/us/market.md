## Protected titles
- USA: engineering regulation is state by state. NCEES's Model Law and Model Rules define the education requirement as an EAC/ABET degree or an equivalent meeting the NCEES Engineering Education Standard, and leave a foreign licensee's comity to each board's judgment (NCEES, « Model Law » (2018), consulted 2026-11-07, S146; NCEES, « Model Rules » (2016), consulted 2026-11-07, S147). Titles vary: Florida bars unlicensed persons from titles tending to indicate an active licence, explicitly listing "software engineer", "computer hardware engineer" and "systems engineer" (Florida Legislature, « Fla. Stat. §471.031, Engineering » (2026), §471.031(1)(b)1, consulted 2026-11-07, S143); Texas restricts the word too (Texas Board of Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors, « PAO-65 response » (2024), consulted 2026-11-07, S21); New York allows only licensed persons to practice engineering "or use the title 'professional engineer'" (New York State Senate, « N.Y. Education Law § 7202 », §7202, consulted 2026-11-07, S165). Rule: never claim a US licence or a licence-implying title; for a specific state, check its board; don't claim a rule for an unchecked state. "PE" needs a state licence (LOW, unsourced). California: B&P Code §§6704, 6732 reserve "professional engineer", "licensed engineer", "registered engineer", "consulting engineer" and the branch titles agricultural, chemical, civil, control system, electrical, fire protection, industrial, mechanical, metallurgical, nuclear, petroleum and traffic engineer to licensees; §§6736, 6736.1 add "structural engineer" and "soil/soils/geotechnical engineer"; §6787(g) makes it a misdemeanor to use "any other title" that suggests practising civil, electrical or mechanical engineering, and (i) to use the listed titles or an abbreviation "of that title" that might suggest licensure, unlicensed (California BPELSG, « Professional Engineers Act (Bus. & Prof. Code §§6700-6799) » (2026), consulted 2026-11-08, S592). "Software engineer" and "systems engineer" are not in the lists and BPELSG's FAQ lists no software branch (California BPELSG, « Application and Licensing FAQs », Q18, consulted 2026-11-07, S145). Not listed, but no source says they are allowed (LOW, unsourced). §6747 does not mention titles; §§6740 and 6746.1 keep the §6732 titles restricted for the subordinates and communications employees they cover (California BPELSG, « Professional Engineers Act (Bus. & Prof. Code §§6700-6799) » (2026), consulted 2026-11-08, S592); for industrial-corporation employees under §6747 the title question is unaddressed (LOW, unsourced).
