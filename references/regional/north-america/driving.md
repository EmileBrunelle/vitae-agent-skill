# Regulated professions: commercial driving licences (US CDL, Canada/Québec)

Loaded on demand when the job requires driving a heavy or commercial vehicle and the candidate lists a driver credential. Québec classes (1, 2, 3, 5; mentions F, M, T): [`quebec/trades.md`](quebec/trades.md) (SAAQ). A licence is issued by a government for a jurisdiction; it is not a training card. Each fact is what the regulator's own page or text says (consulted 2026-11-07; the Canadian and Québec licence sources, 2026-11-08).

## Who issues what

- "FMCSA does not issue Commercial Driver's Licenses (CDLs). State governments are responsible for issuing CDLs" (FMCSA, « Commercial Driver's License Program », snapshot 2026-10-24, S452). Name the state in the CV line; the licence number is not needed (suggested).
- A CDL is "a license issued to an individual by a State or other jurisdiction of domicile ... which authorizes the individual to operate a class of a commercial motor vehicle" (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.5, Definitions », consulted 2026-11-07, S453). A "commercial learner's permit (CLP)" only allows accompanied behind-the-wheel training: a CLP is not a CDL (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.5, Definitions », consulted 2026-11-07, S453).
- Every CDL shows its date of issue and expiry and the group and endorsements (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.153, Information on the CLP and CDL documents and applications », consulted 2026-11-07, S454): print validity as [`../../certifications/rules.md`](../../certifications/rules.md) says, with the dates on the licence. Federal text sets no period in the sections read.

## Class (group) and endorsements

- Federal text mostly says "group": "Combination vehicle (Group A)", "Heavy Straight Vehicle (Group B)", "Small Vehicle (Group C)", shown on the CDL as A, B, C (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.91, Commercial motor vehicle groups », consulted 2026-11-07, S455; eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.153, Information on the CLP and CDL documents and applications », consulted 2026-11-07, S454); the ELDT rule and some restriction codes write "Class A CDL", "Class A passenger vehicle" (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 380.603 and 380.605 », consulted 2026-11-07, S456; eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.153, Information on the CLP and CDL documents and applications », consulted 2026-11-07, S454). Write "Class A CDL" or "Group A" as the state licence reads.
- Group A: combination with a GCWR of 26,001 lb or more where the towed unit exceeds 10,000 lb GVWR. Group B: a single vehicle of 26,001 lb or more, or one towing no more than 10,000 lb. Group C: neither, but designed for 16 or more passengers including the driver, or used to transport hazardous materials as § 383.5 defines them (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.91, Commercial motor vehicle groups », consulted 2026-11-07, S455).
- A Group A holder who passed the tests "may operate a heavy straight vehicle (Group B) or a small vehicle (Group C), provided that he/she possesses the requisite endorsement(s)"; Group B covers Group C the same way (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.91, Commercial motor vehicle groups », consulted 2026-11-07, S455). Do not write "Class B" as extra when the CDL is Class A.
- Endorsement codes: T double/triple trailers, P passenger, N tank vehicle, H hazardous materials, X tank and hazardous materials combined, S school bus (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.153, Information on the CLP and CDL documents and applications », consulted 2026-11-07, S454). Each is earned by tests: knowledge test for T, N, H; knowledge and skills test for P and S (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.93, Endorsements », consulted 2026-11-07, S457). An endorsement the licence does not show is not held.
- Restrictions on the licence (for example L "No Air brake equipped CMV", E "No Manual transmission equipped CMV", O "No Tractor-trailer CMV") limit what the CV may claim: do not list a vehicle type or transmission the restriction code excludes (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.153, Information on the CLP and CDL documents and applications », consulted 2026-11-07, S454).

## Training is not the licence

- Entry-level driver training (ELDT) applies to an "entry-level driver": one who must pass the CDL tests for a first CDL, an upgrade to Class A or B, or a first P, S or H endorsement, with listed exceptions (for example military CMV experience, restricted CDLs). Drivers issued a Class A or B CDL or a P, S or H endorsement before February 7, 2022 are not required to comply for that CDL or endorsement; those who obtain a CLP on or after that date are. Training must come from "an entity listed on FMCSA's Training Provider Registry" (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 380.603 and 380.605 », consulted 2026-11-07, S456). Completed training is a course: print "CDL training, <provider>, <year>" (suggested), not as the licence.

## Cross-border

- A footnote to 49 CFR 383.23 records the Administrator's determination that commercial licences issued by Canadian provinces and territories "in conformity with the Canadian National Safety Code" are in accordance with the US standards, and a driver holding one "is prohibited from obtaining a non-domiciled CDL, or any other type of driver's license" from a US state (eCFR (US), « 49 CFR 383.23 », consulted 2026-11-07, S458). Print the Canadian licence as issued (province, class); do not describe it as a "US CDL".
- Canadian provinces other than Québec: no official page loaded; not covered here.

## Canadian and Québec licences

- Québec class 1 (SAAQ): "authorizes its holder to drive a combination of vehicles", a road tractor with trailer(s) or semi-trailer(s), and "all other types of road vehicles, except for" motorcycles (check the exceptions on the page); mentions F "To drive a heavy vehicle equipped with an air brake system" and M "To drive a heavy vehicle equipped with a manual transmission" (SAAQ, « Classes of Driver's Licence », consulted 2026-11-08, S556). Classes and T: [`quebec/trades.md`](quebec/trades.md) (SAAQ, « Classes de permis de conduire », consulted 2026-11-07, S329).
- CV line: `Class 1 driver's licence (Québec, SAAQ), endorsements F, M, valid until <year>`: class, the endorsements the licence shows (omit endorsements the licence does not show), validity as [`../../certifications/rules.md`](../../certifications/rules.md#validity-and-renewal) says. A DEP in truck driving is a course, not the licence (see [`quebec/outbound-canada.md`](quebec/outbound-canada.md#d-highest-credential-is-not-a-bachelors)). Convention (LOW, unsourced).
- Ontario: "If you have a truck driver's licence from another province, your Ontario licence will be the same class" (Government of Ontario, « Get a truck driver's licence », consulted 2026-11-08, S557); an out-of-province licence can be exchanged when relocating from "any Canadian province or territory" (Government of Ontario, « Exchange an out-of-province driver's licence », consulted 2026-11-08, S558). No page read maps Québec class 1 to Ontario AZ: never write "equivalent to AZ" or "AZ equivalent" (rule); if asked, say it is a likely reading of the Ontario sentence, to confirm with the employer or the licensing office (LOW, unsourced: inference from S557). Why no mapping is sourced: the CCMTA says its class system "is used by all the Canadian jurisdictions except Ontario" (CCMTA (Canadian Council of Motor Transport Administrators), « National Safety Code Standard 6: Determining Driver Fitness in Canada, 2026 Update, June 2026 », consulted 2026-11-08, S591; NSC Standard 6, printed p. 4), and the CVSA guide (CVSA (Commercial Vehicle Safety Alliance), « Canadian Driver's Licence Reference Guide », 2025, consulted 2026-11-08, S590) describes the two separately: Ontario Class A, "Any combination of motor vehicle and towed vehicle where towed vehicle exceeds 4,600 kg, but not a bus carrying passengers", with "Z" "Can drive a vehicle equipped with air brakes"; Québec Class 1, "Truck-tractor bearing (two axles and a net weight of 4,500 kg or more) or three or more axles, pulling one or more trailer(s) or semi-trailer(s)", with F "May drive vehicles equipped with an air braking system". Neither source states an equivalence. Neither is a legal source: the CVSA guide is "designed to be used as guidelines only" and "may not conform exactly to current provincial/territorial regulations" (CVSA (Commercial Vehicle Safety Alliance), « Canadian Driver's Licence Reference Guide », 2025, consulted 2026-11-08, S590); no jurisdiction "is legally required to adopt the CCMTA standards" (CCMTA (Canadian Council of Motor Transport Administrators), « National Safety Code Standard 6: Determining Driver Fitness in Canada, 2026 Update, June 2026 », consulted 2026-11-08, S591). Convention (LOW, unsourced): print the licence class as it appears on the licence (`Québec Class 1 licence, F endorsement`); when targeting Ontario, describe the vehicle (tractor-trailer, air brakes) rather than claim AZ.
- Other provinces: no official page loaded. Air-brake handling differs by province, so never generalise "class 1 includes air brakes": in Alberta "The Class 1 licence includes the air brake endorsement" while other classes need a "Q" endorsement (CVSA (Commercial Vehicle Safety Alliance), « Canadian Driver's Licence Reference Guide », 2025, consulted 2026-11-08, S590); in Ontario air brakes are the separate "Z" code (above).
- US: the 49 CFR 383.23 footnote above applies; print the licence as issued (Québec, class 1), never as a "US CDL" or "Class A".
