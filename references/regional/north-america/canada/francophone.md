# Market: francophone Canada outside Québec

Loaded on demand when the CV is French or the employer is francophone in New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, PEI, Ontario or Manitoba (never for a Québec target). Read together with [`north-america/common.md`](../common.md) and [`canada/market.md`](market.md), which still apply. Sources loaded: NB, Ontario, PEI, Manitoba, federal; NS only the posting practice below. Québec is not the French-Canadian case.

## Which `quebec/` rules carry over
- Not outside Québec: OQLF diploma abbreviations, Code des professions, OIQ, MIFI, DEC-BAC ([`quebec/market.md`](../quebec/market.md), [`quebec/education.md`](../quebec/education.md)). No loaded NB, Ontario or Manitoba source sets an equivalent norm: write diploma names in full as the institution prints them (convention; LOW, unsourced).
- Every French CV: the month trap in [`references/ats.md`](../../../ats.md). The infinitive verb style is a Québec career-service convention (`quebec/market.md`); follow the posting.
- Québec credentials: the `quebec/outbound-*` files apply, with their "no equivalences" rule; their gloss targets English readers, so a French CV keeps `DEC, Informatique de Gestion` as is. No loaded source says how NB or Ontario francophone employers read a DEC: do not add a gloss unless the candidate approves.

## Protected titles
- NB: only members, licensees and certificate holders may use « ingénieur », « ing. », « géoscientifique » or « géosc. » or any addition or abbreviation (s. 9); representing oneself as an engineer by title is deemed practice (s. 10(b)); the French text lists no « P. Eng. », which belongs to English CVs (Legislative Assembly of New Brunswick, « Projet de loi 18 / Bill 18, Loi sur les professions d'ingénieur et de géoscientifique », consulted 2026-11-07, S193; 2015 bill text of the Act, not the consolidation). The regulator's French name in its own bilingual by-laws: « Association des ingénieurs et des géoscientifiques du Nouveau-Brunswick » (APEGNB / AIGNB (Association of Professional Engineers and Geoscientists of New Brunswick), « By-Laws / Règlements administratifs pris en vertu de la Loi sur les professions d'ingénieur et de géoscientifique », consulted 2026-11-07, S206).
- Ontario: without a licence it is an offence to use « ingénieur » or "professional engineer" as an occupational designation, "engineer" so as to suggest one may practise engineering, or any term that suggests it (s. 40(2)); French regulator name « Ordre des ingénieurs de l'Ontario » (s. 2(1)) (Government of Ontario (e-Laws; copy hosted by Engineers Canada), « Professional Engineers Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. P.28 », consulted 2026-11-07, S194; Government of Ontario (e-Laws), « Loi sur les ingénieurs, L.R.O. 1990, chap. P.28 », consulted 2026-11-07, S207; consolidation from 2025-01-01, same wording as the 2012 one). « ingénieur » is defined as the holder of a licence or temporary licence (s. 1(1)); using « stagiaire en ingénierie » (SI) or « technologue en ingénierie titulaire de permis » (TITP) in a way that suggests holding that status is an offence too (s. 40(3.1), (3.2)). No French abbreviation for « ingénieur »: write it and the regulator's French name.
- Software-engineer rule: [`canada/market.md`](market.md#protected-titles). No other NB or Ontario regulated title is sourced; use its regulated-occupation check.

## Credentials
- No DEC, AEC or cégep. Secondary: NB as printed on the transcript (CICIC, « Le système d'éducation du Nouveau-Brunswick », consulted 2026-11-07, S204); Ontario « Diplôme d'études secondaires de l'Ontario (DESO) » (Government of Ontario, « Obtenir le diplôme d'études secondaires », consulted 2026-11-07, S208). College level and university: CICIC charts show a generic « diplôme d'études collégiales » (1 to 3 years) and « baccalauréat » (3 to 4 years) for both provinces (CICIC, « Le système d'éducation du Nouveau-Brunswick » (2016), consulted 2026-11-07, S204; CICIC, « Le système d'éducation de l'Ontario » (2016), consulted 2026-11-07, S205); that is CICIC's label, not a credential name.
- Ontario colleges: the ministry's credential validation names « certificat d'études collégiales », « diplôme », « diplôme d'études collégiales – niveau avancé », « certificat postdiplôme » (examples, not exhaustive; Government of Ontario, « Normes des programmes collégiaux » (2025-07-10), consulted 2026-11-07, S209). Collège Boréal prints « Certificat d'études collégiales de l'Ontario » (CECO), « Diplôme d'études collégiales de l'Ontario » (DECO), « … – niveau avancé » (DECAO), « Certificat postdiplôme de l'Ontario » (CPDO); its own « Certificat d'études du Collège Boréal » (CECB) and attestations are not ministry-approved: never present them as CECO (Collège Boréal, « Politique D 08 – Titres de compétences » (rev. 2024-10-18), consulted 2026-11-07, S210). Abbreviations: Boréal only; elsewhere copy the transcript.

## Bilingualism
- NB: French and English are the official languages with equal status in provincial institutions (Government of New Brunswick, « Loi sur les langues officielles, O-0.5 », consulted 2026-11-07, S195). GNB language-services scale (the page ties it to teaching and education jobs):  Basique, Basique Plus, Intermédiaire, Intermédiaire Plus, Avancé, Avancé Plus, Supérieur, mapped to CECR (Intermédiaire B1.1 … Supérieur C1+) (Government of New Brunswick, « Comprendre votre niveau de compétence » (undated), consulted 2026-11-07, S199); no loaded page says which positions require which level. State a level only as an official result or in the posting's own wording; otherwise describe in words ("français et anglais, langues de travail").
- Federal jobs (NB, Ontario, Manitoba): PSC levels A (débutant), B (intermédiaire), C (avancé), one per skill (compréhension de l'écrit, expression écrite, interaction orale), set by the position's profile; external candidates are tested late in the process (Public Service Commission of Canada, « Postes bilingues : foire aux questions » (2017), consulted 2026-11-07, S196); levels per (Public Service Commission of Canada, « Évaluation de langue seconde – Évaluation linguistique à l'oral » (2026-03), consulted 2026-11-07, S197; Public Service Commission of Canada, « Évaluation de langue seconde ─ Test de compréhension de l'écrit » (2026-03), consulted 2026-11-07, S198). No loaded page gives a written notation such as "CBC" or the validity of a result: if the candidate holds results, write each skill with its level and year, e.g. `lecture <A/B/C>`; never invent one.
- Ontario: French Language Services Act designated areas, 25 entries as of 2026-01 (O. Reg. 272/25; 6 expanded in 2025; second list in force 2029) (Government of Ontario, « Government services in French », consulted 2026-11-07, S200); check the municipality there. OPS designated bilingual positions require French orally and/or in writing, the ad shows a "language of position" and candidates are tested in French (Government of Ontario, « Careers: hiring process », consulted 2026-11-07, S201). Levels used in postings: unloaded.
- PEI provincial public service only: a designated bilingual position needs oral French « Avancé » or better on the PEI Public Service Commission scale (not the federal A/B/C), confirmed by an interview with that Commission before the offer; the result is valid two years (PEI Public Service Commission, « HR Manual 8.04 Évaluations de la compétence en français » (rev. 2017-10-04), consulted 2026-11-07, S211). State a level only from that result.
- Manitoba: public entities under the French-language services policy hire only bilingual employees into bilingual service centres and designated bilingual positions, until bilingual staffing needs are met (Government of Manitoba, Secrétariat aux affaires francophones, « Politique sur les services en français » (2017), consulted 2026-11-07, S212); no level scale loaded.
- NS public service: the Public Service Commission works with hiring departments to decide per position whether French is a « requirement » or an « asset » in the posting (Government of Nova Scotia, Public Service Commission, « French Language Services Plan 2024-25 – Public Service Commission » (2024-25), consulted 2026-11-07, S213); no level scale loaded.
