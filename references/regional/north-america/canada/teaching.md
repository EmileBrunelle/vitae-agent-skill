# Regulated professions: teaching licences, Canada outside Québec (Ontario, Alberta)

Loaded on demand when a candidate lists a K-12 teaching certificate issued by a Canadian province other than Québec (Québec: [`../quebec/teaching.md`](../quebec/teaching.md)). Provincial bodies govern K-12 certification (TESL Canada, « Certification FAQ », consulted 2026-11-07, S423); no national licence appeared in the sources read, which cover only Ontario and Alberta: for any other province read its regulator's page, do not extend these rules. Adult-ESL certification (TESL Canada) is not a licence: [`../../../certifications/teaching-english.md`](../../../certifications/teaching-english.md). Academic CV: [`../../../fields/academic.md`](../../../fields/academic.md). Rule 1 applies.

## Ontario (Ontario College of Teachers, OCT)
- Exact title: « Certificate of Qualification and Registration » (CQR), "your licence to teach in Ontario's publicly funded schools"; "You must pay an annual membership fee to keep your certificate in good standing" (Ontario College of Teachers, « How We Certify Teachers », consulted 2026-11-07, S424). It has four sections: Degree(s), Program of Teacher Education, Basic Qualifications, Additional Qualifications. Additional Qualifications (AQ) show on the certificate "the calendar year after they are completed" (Ontario College of Teachers, « How We Certify Teachers », consulted 2026-11-07, S424): a recent AQ may not be printed yet.
- Transitional forms: the TCQR is valid 18 months (one 6-month extension), the MTCQR six years (one 1-year extension) (Ontario College of Teachers, « How We Certify Teachers », consulted 2026-11-07, S424). The College says their holders "are Ontario Certified Teachers (OCT)", eligible to teach "as long as they maintain their membership in good standing" (Ontario College of Teachers, « Transitional Certificates », consulted 2026-11-07, S425). Print the exact form, not a plain CQR.
- The designation is law. College Act, s. 14(6): "No person except a member of the College in good standing shall use the English title 'Ontario Certified Teacher' or the French title 'Enseignant(e) agréé(e) de l'Ontario' or an abbreviation of any of those titles to describe themselves or their profession." s. 14(7): good standing means the member "has paid their annual membership fee and holds a valid certificate of qualification and registration that is not revoked, suspended, or cancelled" (Government of Ontario (e-Laws), « Ontario College of Teachers Act, 1996, S.O. 1996, c. 12, s. 14 », consulted 2026-11-07, S426). s. 14(9) also bars anyone but a member from holding out as a member (Government of Ontario (e-Laws), « Ontario College of Teachers Act, 1996, S.O. 1996, c. 12, s. 14 », consulted 2026-11-07, S426).
- Rule: print "OCT" (or the full title) only while the candidate is in good standing. Lapsed, expired, cancelled or surrendered: do not print it as current; print the qualification earned with dates ("Ontario teacher certification, 2016–2023"; suggested wording) and flag it. No page loaded prescribes wording for a lapsed holder; this is a reading of s. 14(6)-(7).
- Inactive: "Ontario Certified Teacher — Inactive/Non-Practising" is a separate protected title, for a member suspended solely under clause 24(1)(a) (s. 14(8)); the Act notes a pending amendment (2023, c. 11, Sched. 3, s. 2) tying it to by-law criteria (Government of Ontario (e-Laws), « Ontario College of Teachers Act, 1996, S.O. 1996, c. 12, s. 14 », consulted 2026-11-07, S426). The College says that status "may not teach in publicly funded schools" and that the designation is for members "no longer active" (Ontario College of Teachers, « Glossary », consulted 2026-11-07, S427). Never drop "Inactive/Non-Practising".
- Verification: the College's public register is "Find a Teacher"; its glossary lists the membership statuses (Good Standing, Good Standing with Terms, Conditions or Limitations, Inactive/Non-Practising, Expired, Cancelled, Certificate Surrendered, among others) and, as "other entries on the public register", degrees, program and qualifications (Ontario College of Teachers, « Glossary », consulted 2026-11-07, S427). A member "with Terms, Conditions or Limitations" is still authorised to teach; the terms are on the certificate: ask the candidate before printing a plain "OCT".

## Alberta (Alberta Education)
- "To teach in Alberta, you must hold a valid Alberta teaching certificate issued by Alberta Education"; it "is the only body that can assess credentials for teaching authority in Alberta" (Government of Alberta (Alberta Education), « Teacher certification », consulted 2026-11-07, S428).
- Exact titles: « Interim Professional Certification » ("valid for 3 years") then « Permanent Professional Certification », which a teacher cannot apply for: the school authority must recommend it after 2 years of full-time teaching in an Alberta accredited school on the interim certificate and 2 successful formal evaluations. It "does not expire, even if you leave the profession" (Government of Alberta (Alberta Education), « Teacher certification », consulted 2026-11-07, S428).
- Print which one is held. An interim certificate has an end date: print it (or the new one after an extension or re-issuance). Never write "Permanent" for an interim holder.
- A 90-day "temporary teaching authority" exists for graduates with an offer ahead of graduation, and a "Letter of Authority" for some labour-mobility applicants (Government of Alberta (Alberta Education), « Teacher certification », consulted 2026-11-07, S428): no source calls either a certificate; print each by its exact name, never as a teaching certificate.
- Verification: the Teacher and Teacher Leader Registry lists certificated and former teachers back to 1954, with certificate type, issue date and status, and decisions on suspensions and cancellations. Pending or granted exemption requests keep information out of it (Government of Alberta, « Teacher and Teacher Leader Registry », consulted 2026-11-07, S429): absence proves nothing.

## Between provinces
- Alberta says teachers holding a valid certificate from another Canadian jurisdiction "are eligible for certification in Alberta without requiring additional education, training or examination" under the Canadian Free Trade Agreement, but "must still apply" and may receive an Interim Professional Certification or a Letter of Authority (Government of Alberta (Alberta Education), « Teacher certification », consulted 2026-11-07, S428). Québec: [`../quebec/teaching.md`](../quebec/teaching.md). List each province's certificate separately; never merge them into "Canadian teaching licence".
