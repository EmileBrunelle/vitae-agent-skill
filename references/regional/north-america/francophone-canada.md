# Market: francophone Canada outside Québec

Add-on to [`north-america/us-canada.md`](us-canada.md), which still applies. Sources loaded: NB, Ontario, PEI, Manitoba, federal; NS only the posting practice below. Québec is not the French-Canadian case.

## Which `quebec/` rules carry over
- Not outside Québec: OQLF diploma abbreviations, Code des professions, OIQ, MIFI, DEC-BAC ([`quebec/market.md`](quebec/market.md), [`quebec/education.md`](quebec/education.md)). No loaded NB, Ontario or Manitoba source sets an equivalent norm: write diploma names in full as the institution prints them (convention, LOW confidence).
- Every French CV: the month trap in [`references/ats.md`](../../ats.md). The infinitive verb style is a Québec career-service convention (`quebec/market.md`); follow the posting.
- Québec credentials: the `quebec/outbound-*` files apply, with their "no equivalences" rule; their gloss targets English readers, so a French CV keeps `DEC, Informatique de Gestion` as is. No loaded source says how NB or Ontario francophone employers read a DEC: do not add a gloss unless the candidate approves.

## Protected titles
- NB: only members, licensees and certificate holders may use « ingénieur », « ing. », « géoscientifique » or « géosc. » or any addition or abbreviation (s. 9); representing oneself as an engineer by title is deemed practice (s. 10(b)); the French text lists no « P. Eng. », which belongs to English CVs (S193, 2015 bill text of the Act, not the consolidation). The regulator's French name in its own bilingual by-laws: « Association des ingénieurs et des géoscientifiques du Nouveau-Brunswick » (S206).
- Ontario: without a licence it is an offence to use « ingénieur » or "professional engineer" as an occupational designation, "engineer" so as to suggest one may practise engineering, or any term that suggests it (s. 40(2)); French regulator name « Ordre des ingénieurs de l'Ontario » (s. 2(1)) (S194 and S207, consolidation from 2025-01-01, same wording as the 2012 one). « ingénieur » is defined as the holder of a licence or temporary licence (s. 1(1)); using « stagiaire en ingénierie » (SI) or « technologue en ingénierie titulaire de permis » (TITP) in a way that suggests holding that status is an offence too (s. 40(3.1), (3.2)). No French abbreviation for « ingénieur »: write it and the regulator's French name.
- Software-engineer rule: `us-canada.md`. No other NB or Ontario regulated title is sourced; use its regulated-occupation check.

## Credentials
- No DEC, AEC or cégep. Secondary: NB as printed on the transcript (S204); Ontario « Diplôme d'études secondaires de l'Ontario (DESO) » (S208). College level and university: CICIC charts show a generic « diplôme d'études collégiales » (1 to 3 years) and « baccalauréat » (3 to 4 years) for both provinces (S204, S205, 2016); that is CICIC's label, not a credential name.
- Ontario colleges: the ministry's credential validation names « certificat d'études collégiales », « diplôme », « diplôme d'études collégiales – niveau avancé », « certificat postdiplôme » (examples, not exhaustive; S209, 2025-07-10). Collège Boréal prints « Certificat d'études collégiales de l'Ontario » (CECO), « Diplôme d'études collégiales de l'Ontario » (DECO), « … – niveau avancé » (DECAO), « Certificat postdiplôme de l'Ontario » (CPDO); its own « Certificat d'études du Collège Boréal » (CECB) and attestations are not ministry-approved: never present them as CECO (S210, rev. 2024-10-18). Abbreviations: Boréal only; elsewhere copy the transcript.

## Bilingualism
- NB: French and English are the official languages with equal status in provincial institutions (S195). GNB language-services scale (the page ties it to teaching and education jobs):  Basique, Basique Plus, Intermédiaire, Intermédiaire Plus, Avancé, Avancé Plus, Supérieur, mapped to CECR (Intermédiaire B1.1 … Supérieur C1+) (S199, undated); no loaded page says which positions require which level. State a level only as an official result or in the posting's own wording; otherwise describe in words ("français et anglais, langues de travail").
- Federal jobs (NB, Ontario, Manitoba): PSC levels A (débutant), B (intermédiaire), C (avancé), one per skill (compréhension de l'écrit, expression écrite, interaction orale), set by the position's profile; external candidates are tested late in the process (S196, 2017); levels per S197 and S198 (2026-03). No loaded page gives a written notation such as "CBC" or the validity of a result: if the candidate holds results, write each skill with its level and year, e.g. `lecture <A/B/C>`; never invent one.
- Ontario: French Language Services Act designated areas, 25 entries as of 2026-01 (O. Reg. 272/25; 6 expanded in 2025; second list in force 2029) (S200); check the municipality there. OPS designated bilingual positions require French orally and/or in writing, the ad shows a "language of position" and candidates are tested in French (S201). Levels used in postings: unloaded.
- PEI provincial public service only: a designated bilingual position needs oral French « Avancé » or better on the PEI Public Service Commission scale (not the federal A/B/C), confirmed by an interview with that Commission before the offer; the result is valid two years (S211, rev. 2017-10-04). State a level only from that result.
- Manitoba: public entities under the French-language services policy hire only bilingual employees into bilingual service centres and designated bilingual positions, until bilingual staffing needs are met (S212, 2017); no level scale loaded.
- NS public service: the Public Service Commission works with hiring departments to decide per position whether French is a « requirement » or an « asset » in the posting (S213, 2024-25); no level scale loaded.
