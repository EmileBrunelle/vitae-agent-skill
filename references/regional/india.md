# Market: India

India only (private sector, public sector/PSU, campus and lateral hiring). Thinly sourced: official bodies publish little CV guidance, so most custom rules below are labelled LOW confidence; ask the user which segment they target.

Candidate with Indian credentials targeting another market: also read [`india-outbound.md`](india-outbound.md).

## At a glance
- Paper: a4.
- Photo: not required by any source found; Naukri's own resume template still carries a passport-size photograph placeholder (Naukri, « Resume template », n.d.). Tell the user the custom; the candidate decides. Default omit for private-sector/MNC/ATS targets (LOW confidence).
- CV language: English for private sector and MNCs. Government/PSU postings may require an application form in the posting's language, not a free-form CV.
- Personal data: private sector, omit all but contact. Government/PSU and some campus formats use fuller personal details (father's name, marital status, gender) (LOW confidence). Never a national ID (Aadhaar, PAN) or passport number.
- Formality / verb style: English; no India-specific verb convention found.
- Length: 1 page fresher/campus, 2 pages experienced (LOW confidence; no authoritative source).

## Resume vs biodata
- « Resume »/« CV » and « biodata » are used loosely. A biodata is the older, personal-details-first format (birth date, parents, marital status, religion, height). Build a resume unless the posting or an application form explicitly asks for a biodata or a declaration (LOW confidence on the boundary).
- Naukri's template ends with a « Declaration » (details correct, employer may verify; date, place, signature) and a Personal Details block (date of birth, nationality, addresses) (Naukri, « Resume template », n.d.). Treat both as optional legacy blocks: include only when the target expects them and the candidate agrees; a declaration adds nothing for ATS.

## Degree names and grading
- Naukri's template lists education from the highest qualification down to Class XII and Class X, each with board/university and percentage or CGPA (Naukri, « Resume template », n.d.). Campus and fresher CVs routinely keep Class X and XII results; experienced CVs drop them.
- B.E. vs B.Tech.: both are four-year engineering bachelor's degrees; the name is the awarding university's choice, and AICTE leaves equivalence to the recruiting agency (Careers360, « AICTE clarifies stand on degree equivalence in appointments », 2020-11-02; secondary source, LOW confidence). Write the exact name on the diploma and never normalise one into the other.
- Grades: write the figure as awarded with its scale (« CGPA 8.4/10 » or « 78% »). Do not convert CGPA to percentage yourself: universities use different formulas (the common « CGPA × 9.5 » is a convention, not a confirmed UGC rule; LOW confidence). Use the formula printed by the candidate's university, or leave the original figure.

## Protected titles
- Chartered Accountant / « CA »: only a member of ICAI may use the designation or the « CA » prefix; passing the Final examination alone does not qualify (ICAI, « Pre-conditions for using the designation Chartered Accountant », 2009-08-07; ICAI, « Use of designation(s) other than the designation of Chartered Accountant », 2015-02-18; both citing Chartered Accountants Act 1949, s. 7). A CA-Final-passed non-member or a student writes « CA Intermediate » / « CA Final (cleared) » only if true, never « CA ».
- Architect: only a registered architect or firm may use the title (landscape and naval architects excepted); contravention is an offence (Architects Act 1972, s. 37, as reproduced by Advocatekhoj, « Architects Act, 1972, s. 37 Prohibition against use of title »). Software, solutions and enterprise architect titles: no source settles these; flag, do not rule.
- « Chartered Engineer (India) » is a style of the Institution of Engineers (India), not a statutory licence; no engineers' title act was found (LOW confidence). « Engineer » in a job title is not restricted; do not write « Chartered Engineer » unless the candidate holds it.
- Other councils (NMC, Bar Council) regulate titles by their own acts; not sourced here, so check the council before writing a regulated-sounding title.

## Application package
- CTC and notice period: Indian applications and recruiter screening commonly ask for current CTC, expected CTC and notice period (LOW confidence; search-result summary only). Keep them out of the CV body; give them in the form, cover email or recruiter call, only if the candidate chooses. Never invent them.
- Government/PSU: recruitment goes through the posting's own application form; do not substitute a CV.
