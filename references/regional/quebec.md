# Market: Québec (Canada)

Loaded on demand, in addition to `north-america.md`, only when the target market is Québec (`--region ca` with a French CV, a Québec address, or a Québec employer). The Canada-wide and USA rules stay in `north-america.md`. For a candidate with Québec credentials targeting another market, read `quebec-outbound.md` instead, plus only this file's "Protected titles" section.

## Length and verbs
- Québec French: infinitive action verbs (« Concevoir… », « Livrer… ») — a convention taught by university career services, not a provincial norm; noun-phrase style is equally accepted — don't present it to the user as "the Québec convention". Months lowercase (janv., août); « Juin 2026 – présent », never « Depuis juin 2026 » (not ATS-parseable).
- Québec academic calendars (for bounding year-only education dates, with user confirmation): fall session — cégep starts late August, university early September; winter session — university starts early January, cégep late January; both end in May.

## Protected titles
- Québec reserves professional titles by law. Code des professions, art. 32 (exclusive-practice professions such as ingénieur, architecte) and art. 36 (reserved-title professions): no one may use the title, or "un titre ou une abréviation pouvant laisser croire" they hold it, without the order's permit. Fines start at 2 500 $ for an individual (art. 188); an employer who designates a non-member that way also commits an offence (art. 188.1). Sources: https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/pdf/cs/C-26.pdf
- Engineering: the Loi sur les ingénieurs (art. 22) forbids "le titre d'ingénieur seul ou avec qualificatifs". The OIQ names « ingénieur logiciel », « ingénieur qualité », « ingénieur de produit » as offences even when the employer gave the title, and suggests an unambiguous function title instead. https://www.oiq.qc.ca/publication/usurpation-de-titre-mais-cest-mon-employeur-qui-ma-donne-ce-titre/
- English titles: the 12 Canadian engineering regulators jointly state that "software engineer", "computer engineer" and similar IT titles are restricted to licensed engineers (2022). https://engineerscanada.ca/news-and-events/news/engineering-regulators-reiterate-licensure-requirements-for-those-using-software-engineer-and-other-it-titles
- Rule for the skill: never write, translate or "upgrade" a job title into a reserved one (ingénieur / engineer, architecte, CPA…) unless the candidate confirms membership. If the candidate's real past title contains one and they are not a member, keep the facts but flag it to the candidate and propose a function title (« Développeur logiciel », « Chargé de projets »); never silently rewrite an employer's title.
- « Architecte logiciel / solutions architect »: the Loi sur les architectes (art. 14) forbids any title "susceptible de laisser croire" one practises architecture; no official source settles IT architect titles — flag it, don't rule on it.
- The official list of the 46 regulated professions (it does not say which are reserved-title only): https://www.quebec.ca/emploi/informer-metier-profession/ordres-professionnels/liste-repertoire-membres — link to it, don't copy it.
- A registered candidate may write « candidat à la profession d'ingénieur (CPI) »; same logic for other orders.
- Voluntary designations are not reserved titles: CIPS's I.S.P. is legislated in six provinces but not in Québec (https://cips.ca/?p=3254) — list it under certifications if held.

## Foreign diplomas on a Québec CV
- The MIFI issues an « Évaluation comparative des études effectuées hors du Québec »: an expert opinion that names the Québec academic level the foreign studies compare to — explicitly "not an equivalency" (141 $, target 6 months; since 4 Sept 2025 it no longer compares the field of training, only the level). Sources: https://quebec.ca/emploi/embauche-et-gestion-de-personnel/recruter/embaucher-immigrant/evaluation-diplomes/evaluation-comparative , https://www.quebec.ca/en/immigration/work-quebec/recognition-skills-acquired-abroad/getting-comparative-evaluation/prepare-application
- On the CV: keep the original diploma name and institution, then quote the evaluation's level verbatim, e.g. « Ingeniería en Sistemas, Universidad Central del Ecuador (études comparables à un baccalauréat au Québec — évaluation comparative du MIFI) ». Never write « équivalent ». Without an evaluation, give the program's real duration and suggest the candidate request one.
- Regulated professions: the order decides, through its own procedure; the MIFI evaluation does not replace it, and no reserved title may be used before the permit (see Protected titles).

## Gloss for readers outside Québec
- **CEGEP**, not « Cégep » or « CÉGEP »: in English it's written all-caps, unaccented — native anglophone-cégep usage, not a translation. Never gloss it as "technical college" (not the institution's name, and in North American English it reads as a trade school, collapsing the distinction with the DEP/DVS below) or as "community college" (in the US an alternate/remedial path; in Québec the cégep is the mandatory bridge between high school and university — the analogy undersells the candidate). Same for « BAC+2 »: means nothing to this readership.
- DEC → "Diploma of College Studies (DEC)" — the sanctioned English term; "College Diploma (DEC)" is approximate. The useful distinguishing fact is length: "three-year Career/Technical programs" vs. "two-year Pre-university programs". Recommended descriptor: "CEGEP — Québec public higher education, three-year technical program".
- DEP → "Vocational Diploma (DEP/DVS — Diploma of Vocational Studies)".
- This applies to every credential name, not only the examples listed here — never expand an unfamiliar acronym from memory; verify with the institution or the candidate (rule 1).

## ATS notes
- **Québec DEC / DEP** survive better from a French CV (`DEC, Informatique de Gestion`); from the English CV they become inexact equivalents ("Diploma…", "DVS/DEP").

## Diploma names and abbreviations (OQLF)
- In running text, diploma names are written out in lowercase (« un baccalauréat en informatique »); abbreviations are allowed on a CV. Generic abbreviations: B., M., D., C., L., dipl.; points and a (non-breaking) space between elements, with accents: B. Sc., M. Sc., B. Éd., B. Ing., M. Ing.; sigles without spaces or accents: DEC (or D.E.C.), DEP, AEC, DES; Latin forms: LL. B., Ph. D., M.D.; M.B.A. or MBA admitted. Institution names are proper nouns (« Université Laval »). Source: OQLF BDL, https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/21114/ (2018).
