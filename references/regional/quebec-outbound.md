# Québec credentials, other markets

Loaded on demand, in addition to the target market's file, when the candidate holds Québec credentials (DEC, AEC, DEP, Québec university degree, Québec order membership) and targets ANY market other than Québec.

Also read the "Protected titles" section of [`quebec.md`](quebec.md#protected-titles) (that section only): never write or translate a job title into a reserved one (ingénieur/engineer, architecte, CPA…) without confirmed membership — the same caution applies in every target market.

## Education: name the degree, don't claim equivalences
- Transfers, stopped studies, in-progress degrees, bootcamps: [`references/education-cases.md`](../education-cases.md).
- The Québec path is 11 years of primary and secondary school (6 + 5) + a 2-year pre-university DEC (or 3-year technical DEC) + a bachelor's of typically 3 years (90 credits; at least some engineering bachelor's are 4 years (e.g. 120 credits, génie électrique, Université Laval, « Baccalauréat en génie électrique », program page, consulted 2026-11)) — CICIC (Government of Canada): « Education in Canada: Provinces and Territories of Canada », consulted 2026-11. A Québec baccalauréat is a bachelor's degree: by default write its English name (the university's own English name if it publishes one, e.g. "Bachelor of Science in Computer Science") and nothing more — don't volunteer the program length. No official source states that it "equals" a 4-year degree elsewhere, so never write "equivalent to a 4-year degree" either. Only when the posting or application form explicitly requires a 4-year degree, add the facts once so the reader can do the arithmetic: "(3-year program following a 2-year pre-university DEC)" — for engineering, the program's actual 4 years.
- English names (CICIC): DEC = "Diploma of College Studies", AEC = "Attestation of College Studies". DEP: no official English name confirmed; gloss below.

## English Canada and USA
- An OIQ licence is not valid elsewhere: each province needs its own licence, though the Canadian Free Trade Agreement makes transfer largely automatic (Engineers Canada, « Mobility across Canada », consulted 2026-11). A Canadian licence is not a US PE licence (state boards license; NCEES, « Licensure », consulted 2026-11).
- USA, bare word "engineer": see "Protected titles" in `north-america.md`.
- Engineers targeting the USA: list the Canadian licence as held (« ing. (Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec) » / "P.Eng. (PEO)") and, if the candidate confirms it, the program's CEAB accreditation on the degree line — some states key on them (Texas deems a verified Canadian P.Eng./ing. licence "substantially equivalent to an accredited engineering program": 22 Tex. Admin. Code §133.31(c), amended 2025, consulted 2026-11). Never write "PE" / "Professional Engineer" without a US state licence.
- US credential evaluation (WES, ECE) depends on evaluator and program; ECE: "Not all international degrees are automatically considered equivalent to a U.S. bachelor's degree" (ECE, « How to Get Your Bachelor’s Degree Recognized in the U.S. », consulted 2026-11). Never promise the outcome.
- Work authorization: Canadian citizens can work in the USA under TN status for listed professions (Engineer: bachelor's or provincial licence; 8 CFR 214.6, « Citizens of Canada or Mexico seeking temporary entry under USMCA… », consulted 2026-11). No authoritative source requires stating it on the résumé — ask the candidate; don't add it by default.

## France
- France has no legal diploma equivalence: « on ne reconnaît pas le principe d'équivalence de diplôme sur le plan juridique. On utilise plutôt la notion de comparabilité » (Euroguidance France, May 2025, « La reconnaissance de diplôme », consulted 2026-11). Default for a private-sector, non-regulated job: no ENIC-NARIC « attestation de comparabilité » (the employer judges; Service-public.fr, « Faire reconnaître en France un diplôme obtenu à l’étranger »: « n'a pas de valeur juridique et n'est pas obligatoire », 120 €, answer « dans un délai maximum de 4 mois après le paiement », consulted 2026-11). Write the Québec diploma name + « 1er cycle universitaire, N ans » (and the DEC), no Bac+N figure. Recommend requesting the attestation only when: (1) the candidate targets a French public-sector concours: a candidate lacking the required diploma may be admitted with an equivalent qualification, granted automatically for a diploma of the same level or « après examen de votre dossier par une commission d'équivalence de titres et de diplômes », requested « au moment de votre inscription » (Service-public.fr, « Concours de la fonction publique », consulted 2026-11); that page does not mention the attestation, so ask the organizing authority what to file; (2) the candidate targets large employers whose postings filter on Bac+N (LOW confidence: unsourced); (3) the candidate plans long-term settlement in France (LOW confidence: unsourced). Regulated professions follow their own route (Entente Québec-France ARMs, e.g. engineers OIQ↔CTI; list: Gouvernement du Québec, « Métiers et professions du Québec encadrés par l’Entente Québec-France », consulted 2026-11); diplomas giving access to a regulated profession are excluded from the attestation (Service-public.fr, same page). In France « ingénieur diplômé » is reserved to CTI-accredited diplomas (Gouvernement du Québec, MRIF, « ARM — refonte administrative, ingénieurs »; OIQ↔CTI ARM, 2020 avenant, IESF/CTI/OIQ communiqué « ARM France-Québec 2020 »; consulted 2026-11).
- False friend: in France the baccalauréat ends secondary school; in Québec it is the first university degree (OQLF). On a French CV never write a bare « Baccalauréat en informatique »: give the duration and level wording the reader knows, and if the candidate has an attestation de comparabilité, quote its level verbatim. French postings express education as « Bac+N » (Apec fiche métier, e.g. chef de projet informatique décisionnelle, Apec, « Tous nos métiers : informatique », consulted 2026-11) — only state a Bac+N figure that an attestation (or the candidate's French co-diploma) supports; otherwise give durations.

## Double degrees (Québec–France)
- Double-degree agreements award two separate national diplomas, each issued by its own university (e.g. the UQAR baccalauréat en informatique program + Université de Bretagne Occidentale's Licence en informatique: UBO deliberation 2022-006, art. 1–2, « Vote sur le protocole d’entente spécifique de double-diplôme international Licence d’Informatique (UBO) et Majeure en Informatique UQAR (Canada) », consulted 2026-11). List them as two education entries, each with its institution and city, and mention the agreement once (« double diplomation UQAR–UBO »). Order by the target market: the French diploma first for France (it needs no explanation there), the Québec one first for Québec. Use the exact diploma names from the transcripts (the UQAR program is the baccalauréat en informatique; the diploma awarded is the « Baccalauréat ès sciences appliquées (B. Sc. A.) », UQAR, « Baccalauréat en informatique », consulted 2026-11).

## Gloss for readers outside Québec
- **CEGEP**, not « Cégep » or « CÉGEP »: in English it's written all-caps, unaccented — native anglophone-cégep usage, not a translation. Never gloss it as "technical college" (not the institution's name, and in North American English it reads as a trade school, collapsing the distinction with the DEP/DVS below) or as "community college" (in the US an alternate/remedial path; in Québec the cégep is the mandatory bridge between high school and university — the analogy undersells the candidate). Same for « BAC+2 »: means nothing to this readership.
- DEC → "Diploma of College Studies (DEC)" — the sanctioned English term; "College Diploma (DEC)" is approximate. The useful distinguishing fact is length: "three-year Career/Technical programs" vs. "two-year Pre-university programs". Recommended descriptor: "CEGEP — Québec public higher education, three-year technical program".
- DEP → "Vocational Diploma (DEP/DVS — Diploma of Vocational Studies)".
- This applies to every credential name, not only the examples listed here — never expand an unfamiliar acronym from memory; verify with the institution or the candidate (rule 1).
- ATS: from the English CV, a Québec DEC / DEP becomes an inexact equivalent ("Diploma…", "DVS/DEP"); a French CV keeps `DEC, Informatique de Gestion` intact.
