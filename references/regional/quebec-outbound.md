# Québec credentials, other markets

Loaded on demand, in addition to the target market's file, when the candidate holds Québec credentials and targets a market other than Québec.

Reserved-title rules for Québec orders are in the Protected titles section of `quebec.md`; the same caution applies abroad.

## Education: state facts, not equivalences
- The Québec path is 11 years of secondary + a 2-year pre-university DEC (or 3-year technical DEC) + a bachelor's of typically 3 years (90 credits) — CICIC (Government of Canada): https://www.cicic.ca/1174/postsecondary_education_in_quebec.canada . No official source states that a Québec 3-year bachelor's "equals" a 4-year degree elsewhere: never write "equivalent to a 4-year degree". Write the durations so the reader can do the arithmetic, e.g. "B.Sc. Computer Science (3-year program following a 2-year pre-university DEC)".
- English names (CICIC): DEC = "Diploma of College Studies", AEC = "Attestation of College Studies". (DEP: see the existing gloss; no official English name confirmed.)

## English Canada and USA
- An OIQ licence is not valid elsewhere: each province needs its own licence, though the Canadian Free Trade Agreement makes transfer largely automatic (https://engineerscanada.ca/become-an-engineer/mobility-across-canada). A Canadian licence is not a US PE licence (state boards license; https://ncees.org/licensure/).
- In the USA the bare word "engineer" is regulated by state, not uniformly (e.g. Texas restricts it; https://pels.texas.gov/nm/2024/pao-65-response.pdf). Don't claim a rule for a state without a source; flag it.
- US credential evaluation (WES, ECE) depends on evaluator and program; ECE: "Not all international degrees are automatically considered equivalent to a U.S. bachelor's degree" (https://www.ece.org/Blogs/Bachelors-Degree-Recognized.htm). Never promise the outcome.
- Work authorization: Canadian citizens can work in the USA under TN status for listed professions (Engineer: bachelor's or provincial licence; 8 CFR 214.6, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/214.6). No authoritative source requires stating it on the résumé — ask the candidate; don't add it by default.

## France
- France has no legal diploma equivalence: « on ne reconnaît pas le principe d'équivalence de diplôme sur le plan juridique. On utilise plutôt la notion de comparabilité » (Euroguidance France, May 2025). For a non-regulated job, the employer judges, and may ask for an ENIC-NARIC « attestation de comparabilité » — optional, 120 €, about 4 months; it compares the diploma to « un niveau de diplôme français » (https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F463). Regulated professions follow their own procedure (for Québec: the Entente Québec-France ARMs, e.g. engineers OIQ↔CTI; list: https://www.quebec.ca/emploi/travailler-hors-quebec/faire-reconnaitre-competences-etranger/entente-quebec-france/liste-metiers-professions-quebec). In France « ingénieur diplômé » is reserved to CTI-accredited diplomas.
- False friend: in France the baccalauréat ends secondary school; in Québec it is the first university degree (OQLF). On a French CV never write a bare « Baccalauréat en informatique »: give the duration and level wording the reader knows, and if the candidate has an attestation de comparabilité, quote its level verbatim. French postings express education as « Bac+N » (Apec fiches métier) — only state a Bac+N figure that an attestation (or the candidate's French co-diploma) supports; otherwise give durations.
- « Licence » in France = the 3–4-year first cycle; in Québec the term was replaced by baccalauréat around 1970 (OQLF).

## Double degrees (Québec–France)
- Double-degree agreements award two separate national diplomas, each issued by its own university (e.g. UQAR's Baccalauréat en informatique + Université de Bretagne Occidentale's Licence en informatique: UBO deliberation 2022-006, art. 1–2). List them as two education entries, each with its institution and city, and mention the agreement once (« double diplomation UQAR–UBO »). Order by the target market: the French diploma first for France (it needs no explanation there), the Québec one first for Québec. Use the exact diploma names from the transcripts (UQAR's is « Baccalauréat ès sciences appliquées (B. Sc. A.) »).
