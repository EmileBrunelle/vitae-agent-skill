# Outbound: European credentials → North America

Loaded on demand when the target market is the USA, English Canada or Québec and the candidate's credentials or CV habits are European (France, DACH, Spain, Italy, UK…). Read together with [`north-america/common.md`](../../north-america/common.md) and the target country's file ([`canada/market.md`](../../north-america/canada/market.md) or [`us/market.md`](../../north-america/us/market.md)); for Québec, [`north-america/quebec/market.md`](../../north-america/quebec/market.md). The target market wins over the candidate's address.

## Credential evaluation
- USA: no evaluation is needed to apply. It matters when immigration requires it. 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(C) accepts four alternative routes for an H-1B: a US bachelor's or higher; a foreign degree determined equivalent to a US bachelor's or higher; an unrestricted state licence; or education, training or experience equivalent to that degree plus recognition of expertise in the specialty. Under the last route, USCIS may count "three years of specialized training and/or work experience … for each year of college-level training the alien lacks" (214.2(h)(4)(iii)(D)(5)), which requires evidence of recognition of expertise (U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, « H-1B Specialty Occupations », reviewed 2026-09-21, consulted 2026-11-07, S119). A 3-year Bologna bachelor's is the typical case; DHS notes that « a foreign baccalaureate is not necessarily equivalent to a U.S. baccalaureate » (Federal Register, DHS final rule « Modernizing H-1B Requirements, Providing Flexibility in the F-1 Program, and Program Improvements Affecting Other Nonimmigrant Workers », 89 FR 103054, 2024-12-18, consulted 2026-11-07, S524), but no official text names the 3-year case (LOW, unsourced): the outcome depends on the evaluator and USCIS, never promise it.
- NACES is a trade association of independent US evaluators; it describes the field as operating "In the absence of a US government agency that monitors such services" (NACES, « NACES » (home page), consulted 2026-11-07, S121). A CV never cites an evaluator's conclusion unless the candidate holds the report.
- Canada: the IRCC Educational Credential Assessment (IRCC, « Educational credential assessment », modified 2026-10-16, consulted 2026-11-07, S122; five designated organizations for the general case, plus profession-specific bodies) is required for a principal applicant's eligibility under the Federal Skilled Worker Program, not only for points; it is not an employer requirement. Don't put "ECA" on the CV unless the candidate holds it; then quote its stated Canadian equivalent verbatim.
- Québec: MIFI comparative evaluation, see the "Foreign diplomas on a Québec CV" section of [`north-america/quebec/market.md`](../../north-america/quebec/market.md).

## Degree and grade wording
- Keep the original title and institution; add an English gloss only if the institution publishes that English name, otherwise a plain descriptor the transcript supports.
- Never write "equivalent to a US/Canadian bachelor's/master's" without an evaluation stating it (see the credential section above).
- Grades: the ECTS Users' Guide (European Commission, « ECTS Users' Guide », 2015, § 4.4 « Grade conversion », consulted 2026-11-07, S525) converts grades by comparing grade-distribution tables, the receiving institution deciding, and the German ZAB report carries no grade conversion (ZAB (KMK), « Zeugnisbewertung für ausländische Hochschulabschlüsse », consulted 2026-11-07, S514); no official source covers US GPA evaluators (LOW, unsourced), so don't convert to a GPA; give the original grade with its scale, or omit it.

## Regulated titles
- "Engineer" and « ingénieur » are reserved in Canada: only people licensed by one of the 12 regulators may use the title, "regardless of where they completed their engineering degree" (Engineers Canada, « Immigration and Recognition of Foreign Qualifications: The Role of Engineering Regulators in Canada », 2023, consulted 2026-11-07, S123). Write the degree on the education line and a neutral job title until licensure. Protected-title detail, including software roles: [`north-america/canada/market.md`](../../north-america/canada/market.md#protected-titles).
- Applying with the degree but no licence: describe the degree, not the title. Several Canadian regulators grant an engineer-in-training title (EIT, or Member-in-Training) to enrolled members (Engineers Canada, « Licences issued by the engineering regulators in Canada », undated, consulted 2026-11-26, S665); list it only if the candidate is enrolled with a regulator.
- USA: "PE" needs a state licence; for the bare word "engineer" see [`north-america/us/market.md`](../../north-america/us/market.md#protected-titles).

## CV shape
- Legal anchors on what employers may ask: EEOC: "employers should not ask for a photograph of an applicant"; age and national origin are "irrelevant" to hiring decisions (EEOC, « Prohibited Employment Policies/Practices », consulted 2026-11-07, S125). In Ontario an application form or inquiry that "directly or indirectly classifies an applicant" by a prohibited ground is itself banned (OHRC, « Human Rights at Work 2008, 6. Requesting job-related sensitive information », consulted 2026-11-07, S126); Alberta: employers cannot ask about "family status" or appearance (Alberta Human Rights Commission, « Job advertisements and applications », consulted 2026-11-07, S127). The rules bind employers, not candidates.
- Europass is not the norm in North America (LOW, unsourced: no authoritative source); use the market template in [`north-america/common.md`](../../north-america/common.md). Keep the length rules there: a European 3-page CV gets cut to 1–2 pages.

## Work authorization
- The Alberta Human Rights Commission (« Job advertisements and applications », consulted 2026-11-07, S127) says employers may ask whether a candidate can legally work in Canada, not where they were born. No source requires a status line on the CV: state one only if the candidate confirms the exact status (LOW, unsourced). Never imply citizenship or permanent residence.
- USA: whether to write "requires sponsorship" is a strategy choice with no authoritative guidance; ask the candidate, don't add by default (LOW, unsourced). Never claim "authorized" without a documented basis.
