## Personal data and law
- UK: the Equality Act 2010 protects nine characteristics (s. 4) and applies in Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own equality legislation (LOW, unsourced).
- UK: an employer may not ask about health or disability before offering work or placing the applicant in a pool, except narrow cases (Equality Act 2010, s. 60). Do not volunteer health or disability on the CV; the candidate decides on disclosure.

## Protected titles
- UK engineering: « engineer » alone is not restricted; no licence is needed to practise (Engineering Council, « FAQs: Professional registration », consulted 2026-11-07, S63). CEng, IEng, EngTech are protected by civil law under the Royal Charter and usable only by people on the register (UK Government, « Regulated professions: Chartered Engineer », consulted 2026-11-07, S64).
- UK accountancy: « Chartered Accountant », ACA, FCA (ICAEW), CA (ICAS) and the Chartered Accountants Ireland equivalents are protected titles, with no reserved activity attached (UK Government, « Regulated professions: Chartered Accountant », consulted 2026-11-07, S65). « Accountant » alone is not restricted in UK law (LOW, unsourced).
- Law (England and Wales): « solicitor » is protected by criminal offence for non-solicitors (Solicitors Act 1974, s. 21) (UK Government, « Regulated professions: Solicitor », consulted 2026-11-07, S66).

## Degree names and grading
- UK honours: First (70% and above), 2:1 (60-70%), 2:2 (50-60%), Third (40-50%); taught master's: Distinction, Merit, Pass (Prospects, « Understanding university grades in the UK », September 2026, consulted 2026-11-07, S69). Write the classification as awarded (« First-Class Honours », « 2:1 »).

## Application package
- UK public service: Success Profiles frame civil-service applications; the Scottish Government asks for a CV and a Supporting Statement evidencing the criteria (Scottish Government, « Success profiles: candidate guide », 23 June 2026, consulted 2026-11-07, S71). Other departments and the NHS vary (LOW, unsourced): follow the advert.
