# Outbound: European credentials → North America

Load when the target market is the USA, English Canada or Québec and the candidate's credentials or CV habits are European (France, DACH, Spain, Italy, UK…); the target market wins over the candidate's address. Also read [`north-america/us-canada.md`](../north-america/us-canada.md); for Québec, [`north-america/quebec/market.md`](../north-america/quebec/market.md).

## Credential evaluation
- USA: no evaluation is needed to apply. It matters when immigration requires it. 8 CFR 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(C) accepts four alternative routes for an H-1B: a US bachelor's or higher; a foreign degree determined equivalent to a US bachelor's or higher; an unrestricted state licence; or education, training or experience equivalent to that degree plus recognition of expertise in the specialty. Under the last route, USCIS may count "three years of specialized training and/or work experience … for each year of college-level training the alien lacks" (214.2(h)(4)(iii)(D)(5)), which requires evidence of recognition of expertise (U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, « H-1B Specialty Occupations », reviewed 2026-09-21). A 3-year Bologna bachelor's is the typical case; no loaded official source gives a rule for it (LOW confidence): the outcome depends on the evaluator and USCIS, never promise it.
- NACES is a trade association of independent US evaluators; it describes the field as operating "In the absence of a US government agency that monitors such services" (NACES home page). A CV never cites an evaluator's conclusion unless the candidate holds the report.
- Canada: the IRCC Educational Credential Assessment (IRCC, « Educational credential assessment », modified 2026-10-16: five designated organizations for the general case, plus profession-specific bodies) is required for a principal applicant's eligibility under the Federal Skilled Worker Program, not only for points; it is not an employer requirement. Don't put "ECA" on the CV unless the candidate holds it; then quote its stated Canadian equivalent verbatim.
- Québec: MIFI comparative evaluation, see the "Foreign diplomas on a Québec CV" section of [`north-america/quebec/market.md`](../north-america/quebec/market.md).

## Degree and grade wording
- Keep the original title and institution; add an English gloss only if the institution publishes that English name, otherwise a plain descriptor the transcript supports.
- Never write "equivalent to a US/Canadian bachelor's/master's" without an evaluation stating it (see the credential section above).
- Grades: no loaded official source gives conversion rules and evaluators decide per institution (LOW confidence), so don't convert to a GPA; give the original grade with its scale, or omit it.

## Regulated titles
- "Engineer" and « ingénieur » are reserved in Canada: only people licensed by one of the 12 regulators may use the title, "regardless of where they completed their engineering degree" (Engineers Canada, « Immigration and Recognition of Foreign Qualifications: The Role of Engineering Regulators in Canada », 2023). Write the degree on the education line and a neutral job title until licensure. Protected-title detail, including software roles: [`north-america/us-canada.md`](../north-america/us-canada.md).
- Applying with the degree but no licence: describe the degree, not the title. An engineer-in-training (EIT) status exists; list it only if the candidate is enrolled with a regulator (Engineers Canada, « Who can use the title "engineer"? », 2022-03-24).
- USA: "PE" needs a state licence; for the bare word "engineer" see [`north-america/us-canada.md`](../north-america/us-canada.md).

## CV shape
- Legal anchors on what employers may ask: EEOC: "employers should not ask for a photograph of an applicant"; age and national origin are "irrelevant" to hiring decisions (EEOC, « Prohibited Employment Policies/Practices »). In Ontario an application form or inquiry that "directly or indirectly classifies an applicant" by a prohibited ground is itself banned (OHRC, « Human Rights at Work 2008, 6. Requesting job-related sensitive information »); Alberta: employers cannot ask about "family status" or appearance (Alberta Human Rights Commission, « Job advertisements and applications »). The rules bind employers, not candidates.
- Europass is not the norm in North America (LOW confidence: no authoritative source); use the market template in [`north-america/us-canada.md`](../north-america/us-canada.md). Keep the length rules there: a European 3-page CV gets cut to 1–2 pages.

## Work authorization
- The Alberta Human Rights Commission (same page) says employers may ask whether a candidate can legally work in Canada, not where they were born. No source requires a status line on the CV: state one only if the candidate confirms the exact status (LOW confidence). Never imply citizenship or permanent residence.
- USA: whether to write "requires sponsorship" is a strategy choice with no authoritative guidance; ask the candidate, don't add by default (LOW confidence). Never claim "authorized" without a documented basis.
