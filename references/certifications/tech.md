# Certifications: technology bodies

Loaded with [`rules.md`](rules.md) when the candidate lists a security, cloud, networking, Kubernetes or CompTIA credential. Each fact is what the body's own page says (consulted 2026-11-07) and holds for that credential only; for any other credential ask for the certificate dates. Not read: renewal details for OffSec, CompTIA and Cisco, CKAD and CKS, ISACA's verification tool and mark rules.

## ISC2 (CISSP, SSCP, CCSP, CSSLP, CGRC, ISSAP, ISSEP, ISSMP, CC)
- Certified means experience plus exam: CISSP needs "a minimum of five years cumulative, full-time experience in two or more of the eight domains"; a degree or an approved credential may satisfy up to one year of it (S292).
- Passed without the experience: "Associate of ISC2". Associates "are NOT certified and may not use any Mark or description other than "Associate of ISC2"", on pain of possibly being "prohibited from ever attaining any ISC2 certification" (S296). Write "Associate of ISC2", never "CISSP". The time allowed to earn the experience depends on the exam (CISSP up to six years); the Associate badge does not show which exam was passed, so "passed the CISSP exam" is the candidate's own statement (S301).
- Marks: "Jasmine Domingo, CISSP", not "C.I.S.S.P."; usable on résumés; several marks "from the highest experience to the lowest: ISSAP, ISSEP, ISSMP, CISSP, CCSP, CSSLP, HCISPP, CGRC, SSCP, CC"; not translated. "The Marks are not equivalent to an educational degree, a professional designation, or a title": don't file it under Education (S296, S302).
- A suspended member may "no longer ... imply in any way that they are currently certified" (S296). ISC2 publishes no wording for a former holder: use the dated rule of rules.md.
- Every certification has a Credly URL "that can be embedded on a resume or website"; members control what is public (S296).

## ISACA (CISA)
- The exam may be taken before the experience is met "although you'll have to meet those before getting certified"; 5 years' experience within the 10 years before applying; 5 years from the pass date to apply; 120 CPE hours over 3 years, minimum 20 per year (S293). A passed CISA exam is not "CISA".

## Cloud
- AWS: renewal gives "3 more years of validity", the "maintain" path "1 more year"; "Certification must be active to renew" (S303). Read the per-exam table; don't assume 3 years.
- Google Cloud: Professional valid two years, Foundational and Associate three; "Candidates must recertify in order to maintain their certification status" (S304; levels named on S305).
- Microsoft: renewed annually and free for associate, expert and specialty; "Fundamentals certifications do not expire"; a passed renewal extends "one year from the expiration date" (S299). A Fundamentals line takes the year earned, a role-based line the validity date.
- Linux Foundation CKA: "Certification Valid for 2 Years", with PDF certificate and digital badge (S306).

## Networking and CompTIA
- Cisco CCIE Enterprise Infrastructure: "Your certification is valid for three years"; renewal by continuing-education credits or retaking exams (S307). Print a CCIE number only if the candidate supplies it.
- CompTIA: "The CompTIA certification renewal cycle is three years from the date of certification" (S308).

## OffSec
- Since 2024-11-01 a passed OSCP exam gives two credentials, OSCP+ and OSCP; "OSCP+ ... will expire three (3) years from issuance, whereas your OSCP certification does not expire"; an OSCP passed before that date stays valid for life (S300). Print the one held: "OSCP (2023)" or "OSCP+ (2025–2028)"; no "+" on a pre-2024-11-01 OSCP.
