# Certifications: general rules

Loaded at step 4 whenever the candidate lists a professional certification or designation. Per-body facts: [`tech.md`](tech.md) (security, cloud, networking, Kubernetes), [`finance-accounting.md`](finance-accounting.md) (CPA, CFA, ACCA), [`project-hr.md`](project-hr.md) (PMP, CRHA). A body not covered there: apply these rules and read the body's own usage or trademark page; never generalise one body's rule to another (the per-body rules below differ on purpose).

Rule 1 of SKILL.md applies: print only what the candidate's certificate, badge or the body's record shows.

## Exam passed is not certified
- Several bodies say passing the exam does not grant the credential: ISC2 (experience plus endorsement; the exam-passer is an "Associate of ISC2"), ISACA for CISA (experience "before getting certified"), the CFA Institute (charter depends on work experience and membership), NASBA for the CPA (a state licence) (S292, S293, S294, S295). PMI is the opposite: the pass notification grants the certification (`project-hr.md`).
- Write what is true: "Passed the CISSP exam; Associate of ISC2" or "Passed all three levels of the CFA Program", never the designation. Where the body publishes the wording, copy it.
- Never write a future award or progress after the name ("CFA (expected 2027)"). The CFA Institute prohibits it explicitly; for other bodies it is the same fact problem.
- Course taken without the exam: training, not a certification (see the bootcamp rule in [`../education-cases.md`](../education-cases.md#intensive-non-degree-training-bootcamps)).

## Designation after the name
- Use it only if the body's rules allow it for the candidate's status, in the body's own form: ISC2 "all capital letters and without periods" (S296), PMI "block letters" (S297), CFA "Jane Doe, CFA" (S294).
- Registered-mark symbol: ISC2 wants the ® beside the mark except where size prevents it (S296); the CFA Institute says never "Jane Doe, CFA®" (S294); PMI does not require it after a name (S298). Follow the body.
- Plural: ISC2 ("three (3) CISSPs") and PMI ("PMPs") call it a misuse; the CFA Institute also bars use as a job title (S296, S298, S294). Never write "Senior CFA Analyst".
- ISC2 bars the mark from a business name, domain name and e-mail address (S296).
- Reserved titles (CPA, CRHA, P.Eng.) are legal titles of a professional order, not marks: see [`../regional/north-america/us-canada.md`](../regional/north-america/us-canada.md#protected-titles) and [`../regional/north-america/quebec/market.md`](../regional/north-america/quebec/market.md). A voluntary certification is not a reserved title, but the same test holds: right to use it, true status.

## Validity and renewal
- Print the credential with its validity: "Valid 2024–2027" or "Earned 2022; expired 2025". Lengths differ by body and sometimes by credential within a body; ask for the dates on the certificate or badge.
- A lapsed credential is no longer held: ISC2 ("Use of the Mark must be discontinued immediately if the individual does not maintain their certification") and PMI ("Any individual whose PMI certification has lapsed should immediately discontinue use of the credential") say so (S296, S298). Don't print it as current; if relevant, print it as earned with dates ("CompTIA Security+, 2019–2022 (expired)"). No body prescribes that wording.
- A credential that does not expire stays valid (Microsoft Fundamentals S299, OSCP S300); write the year earned.
- Two credentials from one exam (OffSec OSCP and OSCP+): print the one the candidate holds, with its dates.

## Verification and names
- Print a verification link or number only if the candidate has it and the body issues it; never invent or reformat one. Use official badges as issued: the CFA Institute says "Don't alter, recreate, or redesign a badge" (S294); don't re-draw a badge as an image in the CV. A link is for human readers; whether an ATS reads it is unknown.
- Full official name once with the acronym ("Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP)"), taken from the certificate or the body's page, never from memory (SKILL.md rule 1). Both forms then exist as keywords; the acronym alone is fine afterwards.
- Order: by relevance to the target job, then date; no prestige ranking. Only where the body publishes a hierarchy, follow it: ISC2 lists multiple marks "from the highest experience to the lowest" (S296).
