# Education cases

Loaded on demand at step 4 when the candidate's education includes a degree in progress, studies stopped without the diploma, a transfer or program change, credit recognition, or non-degree intensive training (bootcamp). Never read otherwise. When the target market is Québec, Québec wordings and credentials (DEC-BAC, AEC) are in `regional/quebec.md`, section Education cases.

Universal rule: the CV never implies a diploma the candidate does not hold. Write what was earned, where, and when; let durations and credit counts carry the weight. A diploma name is the institution's exact name (from the transcript or the program page), never a paraphrase or an upgrade.

## Degree in progress
- Give the expected date in place of the end date, month + year. English: "expected grad date (e.g. June 20xx)" (Penn Career Services, https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/resume/).
- Put it first in the section.
- ATS handling of "expected" dates: no vendor documentation found, so don't claim a parsing behaviour.

## Studies stopped without the diploma
- Label it as studies, not as the degree, and add the credits earned in parentheses if relevant. Dates: a range, or the year studies stopped.
- It may be omitted if it has no link with the target job (ask the candidate), but never stretch another entry's dates to cover the gap.
- Don't add a reason for stopping unless the candidate wants one.

## Transfer between institutions or programs
- The degree-granting institution is the education entry. A previous institution is listed as studies (never as a diploma) with its own date range when relevant to the target job. Penn: transfer schools "formatted consistently with school name, location, and dates" (https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/resume/).
- Credit recognition goes in parentheses on the previous institution's line, e.g. "Studies in Software Engineering — University A · 2018–2022 (60 credits recognized toward the B.Sc. at University B)".
- Program change (same institution or not): one entry per diploma obtained, naming the program completed; an abandoned program follows the studies-stopped rule (a sub-line when relevant). If both programs were completed, list both diplomas normally.
- Never merge two institutions into one entry or one date range.

## Intensive non-degree training (bootcamps)
- Write an official credential exactly as issued. A private bootcamp without an official credential is training, never a diploma: program name, provider, dates, real duration (weeks, hours).
- Section (Education vs Additional training): no authoritative source settles it, so ask the candidate and state the choice. Projects built during the program go under Projects.
